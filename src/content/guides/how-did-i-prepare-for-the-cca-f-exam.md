## How do I study inside Claude Academy?

### 1. I went through the courses fast, on purpose

Instead of watching every video, I read through the notes. I did not digest every line on the first pass. I skimmed the notes, then went straight to the quizzes.

### 2. I answered the quizzes from what I actually know

No looking things up mid-quiz. I answered from stock knowledge, or from my best reasoning about what the right answer should be. The point is not to score high. It's to find out what I actually know and what I don't. If I get one wrong, that is useful. It tells me plainly, "you don't know this yet." No shame in that. That is the whole reason I'm studying this way.

### 3. I also built a Claude Code skill that acted as my personal trainer

I built a custom Claude Code skill (think of it as a reusable assistant I set up once) and loaded it with the real exam material:

- the official exam guide
- the GitHub repos people have put together about this certification
- sample scenarios the exam is built around

Because it only knows the verified material, it did not teach me from guesswork.

### 4. I deliberately deferred the hands-on coding that time

The programming activities and exercises, I left for later since I'm slow with the technical, build-it-yourself parts, because I know it will slow the whole thing to a crawl.

So I finished the courses, the quizzes, and the final exam first, then came back and did the technical work properly when I can give it real time.

### 5. Then I studied by hardening my own real system, not example exercises

I used the system I already run every day as my lab.

For each concept the courses cover, I'll find where it already lives in my system, figure out whether it's working, broken, or missing, then understand it and fix it.

I used Claude to point me to the exact lesson to review for whatever I'm working on.

## Why I think this fits this cert

Back in 2020, during the pandemic, I got Facebook Blueprint certification as Creative Strategist. During my self-study, I focused on the practice questions and the list of scenario-based questions they gave. I passed the certification after 1 take.

Every year I have to renew it. The renewal exam is easy as long as I focus on the scenario-based questions.

The pattern is, all the choices all look right, and one underlying concept, a subtle term, or a small distinction is what makes one wrong (unlike the quizzes, where the correct answer is too obvious).
