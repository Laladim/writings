A reader asked me, "How do I make a client trust me on the first call?"

I was exactly like this when I was starting out. I sat in front of every interview wondering the same thing.

Nine out of ten interviews fail because the freelancer is doing the wrong things. Here are three ways to do them right.

**Tip 1: Understand what the client actually wants.**

Understanding the client means you understand their goal, values, mission, the overall soul of their company.

The client will forget you unless the two of you actually connect.

When they ask, "Tell me about yourself" the typical answer goes, "I was a, I worked as, I did this and that." That is a resume read out loud.

The better answer goes, "I love. I am deeply passionate about." Talk about what you care about, not just what you have done.

**Tip 2: Conviction and direction.**

Twenty percent of what you say. Eighty percent of how you say it.

It comes down to how well you know the topic and how well you deliver it.

Position yourself as the expert. They are the ones who need your help. Not the other way around.

**Tip 3: Be someone they can trust and easy to work with.**

They want someone who can properly cover the task from A to Z with less hassle.

Put your arms around their problem and make them feel that you actually care about it. That you will not disappear after the call. That you will not need to be chased for updates. That you will tell them honestly if something is going wrong.

Your takeaway: focus on what you are really, really good at. Grow your experience. Offer it to those who need it. Whether for free at the start, or for a cheap trade. Just go and build it now.

You don't dig the well when you're thirsty.

Less talking, more action.
