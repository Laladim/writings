I had been yellow for twenty years.

Yellow skin. Yellow eyes. Hereditary Spherocytosis. The doctor said the long word the year I was born. My three brothers had the same one. Every year we got blood transfusions. Every year my side hurt. I grew up thinking yellow was my color.

The mirror at home was a small one above the sink. I would brush my teeth and see her every morning. Yellow girl. Yellow eyes. I thought she had always been ugly. I thought that was just how I was made.

The voice in my head said:

"This is your face. You have looked like this since you were a child. There is nothing here you have not already seen. Stop looking."

If you have looked in a mirror and not recognized the person looking back, I have been there.

This year my spleen turned on me. My gallbladder followed. The doctor said the surgery had to happen. Or I would die from poisoning. I was a senior in college. I was scared I would lose my scholarship. I was scared I would not graduate. I was scared AJ was watching me become more sick.

AJ was not watching me become more sick. AJ was sleeping on cardboard. Under my bed. At the PGH charity ward. He was not even my husband yet. He was a boy who was courting me. He went to OJT in the morning. He came to the hospital after. He slept on cardboard. Every night.

The surgery was long. When I woke up my body felt like it belonged to someone else. They told me to rest. They told me the spleen was out. They told me the gallbladder was out. I closed my eyes again.

Some hours later I looked at my hands.

They were pink.

I did not understand for a moment. I turned my hands over. I looked at the palms. I looked at the back of my hands. Pink. Not yellow. Pink.

I asked for a mirror. Someone brought me one. My cheeks were pink. My eyes were not yellow anymore.

I wept. I could not stop. I held the mirror in my hand. I cried so hard my chest hurt.

So this is what I really look like.

I am actually beautiful.

For twenty years I had thought God had made a mistake making me. I had said it to Him in the dark of my bedroom. I had said it many times. He used twenty years of yellow eyes to strip away everything but Himself. I did not know that then. I only knew that I had been wrong about my own face.

If you have been telling yourself God made a mistake. When He made you. Please listen. He did not. The mirror you have been using is small. The light you have been using is dim. A day is coming. You will see yourself the way He has always seen you. You are not a mistake. You are held.

Tonight, before you sleep, do not check the mirror. Close your eyes. Ask God what He sees. Then let Him answer. The next mirror comes when it comes.

For the glory of God.

— Lala
