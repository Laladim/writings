New to the VA world? Start here.

The short version: a virtual assistant is someone who does office work remotely for a client, usually in another country. Calendar management, inbox cleanup, customer replies, data entry, bookkeeping, social media, order processing. If you have done office work anywhere, you have almost certainly done VA work already. You just called it your job.
I have no VA experience. Is this really for me?

Yes, that is exactly who this is for. Almost nobody arrives with VA experience on the page. What you arrive with is office work, customer work, or coordination work done under a different job title, and the rewrite is what makes that visible to a client who has never heard of your employer.

I am not in the Philippines. Can I still use this?

Yes. The converter reads your experience, not your passport, and every rule is the same for everyone: one role, nothing invented, markers where only you know the answer. One caution: the matched-jobs guide draws on our job board, which was built for applicants in the Philippines, so check each posting's location rule before you apply.

Will you invent experience to make me look good?

Never. But we do rewrite, and that distinction matters. The words change so a US client understands what you did and so your resume shows up when they search. The facts never change. Nothing is added that you did not do, and anything you have not done yet is labelled rather than hidden.

Why only one role?

Because a resume aimed at three roles convinces nobody. Most people tick three roles on the form, and that is normal, but the page has to pick one. Your evidence decides which. The other roles do not disappear, they become the future roles your learning path prepares you for.

Do I get a finished resume?

You get a complete draft with markers in it. Some lines only you can write, like how many customers you handled per day or the name of the system you used. Rather than invent a plausible number, the draft asks you for it. You answer, you edit, you send. It has to be yours, in your words, defensible by you in front of a client.

Is it really free? What is the catch?

Yes, free, with no paid course, no upsell and no payment step at the end. Nobody asks you for money at any point, and the form never asks for bank details, IDs or passwords. The catch is the honest one: the draft arrives with markers only you can fill, and you finish it and apply yourself.

When will it arrive, and how do I know the email is really from you?

Packages are built in batches. When yours is built it is sent the same day. It arrives as one email from Lala with six Google Docs links and no attachments. It never asks you to pay, to log in, or to open an attachment. If an email about your resume asks for any of those, it is not from us.

Can I do this on my phone?

Yes. The form works on a phone, and the six documents open on a phone. If the resume upload fails inside the Facebook app, open the link in Chrome or Safari instead. Editing the final draft is easier on a laptop, but it is not required.

Why do you ask about my internet and laptop?

Because VA employers always do. Most job posts require a minimum connection speed and a backup plan for outages. Putting your real setup on your resume answers the question before it is asked, and if your setup needs an upgrade, your plan will say so honestly.

What happens to my resume and my information?

Sensitive personal details are deleted at intake, before any document is written. The rest is exactly what the form's consent line says: "my resume and answers will be stored privately and used only to create my VA resume package." Nothing is posted publicly, and nothing is sent to any employer. You apply to jobs yourself.

Why is the form sometimes closed?

Intake closes whenever processing or delivery needs a safety check. That prevents new submissions from entering an uncertain system. It reopens only after the full converter path passes its verification checks.
