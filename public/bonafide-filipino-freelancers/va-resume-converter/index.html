The four things we are allowed to do

Your resume gets rewritten. That is the point. But only four moves are permitted, and every one of them stays true.

1Reframe what you already didCollections officer at a bank? You handled difficult customer conversations under policy pressure. That is real, and US clients pay for it. It only needed the right words.
2Write a gap as a gapNot "eager to learn QuickBooks", which wastes the line. Instead: the invoicing and expense records you already do in spreadsheets, then the same workflows named inside the tool you have not used yet.
3Earn the lineEvery gap comes with a practice task and the exact sentence you may add to your resume the day you finish it. Never before you have done the work.
4Narrow a title that reads too bigIf your job title carries a clinical, financial or legal word that your actual tasks did not include, one plain line says so, so you are not caught explaining it in the interview.
What is never permitted: invented roles, employers, tools, dates, numbers, or competence. These words are banned from your resume outright, because every one of them reads as "I can do this now": currently building, actively learning, familiar with, working knowledge of, exposure to. A client finds out in the trial task, and you carry that, not us. A resume you cannot defend is worse than no resume.

New to the VA world? Start here.

The short version: a virtual assistant is someone who does office work remotely for a client, usually overseas. Calendar management, inbox cleanup, customer replies, data entry, bookkeeping, social media, order processing. If you have done office work in the Philippines, you have almost certainly done VA work already. You just called it your job.
I have no VA experience. Is this really for me?

Yes, that is exactly who this is for. Almost nobody arrives with VA experience on the page. What you arrive with is office work, customer work, or coordination work done under a different job title, and the rewrite is what makes that visible to a client who has never heard of your employer.

Will you invent experience to make me look good?

Never. But we do rewrite, and that distinction matters. The words change so a US client understands what you did and so your resume shows up when they search. The facts never change. Nothing is added that you did not do, and anything you have not done yet is labelled rather than hidden.

Why only one role?

Because a resume aimed at three roles convinces nobody. Most people tick three roles on the form, and that is normal, but the page has to pick one. Your evidence decides which. The other roles do not disappear, they become the future roles your learning path prepares you for.

Do I get a finished resume?

You get a finished draft with markers in it. Some lines only you can write, like how many customers you handled per day or the name of the system you used. Rather than invent a plausible number, the draft asks you for it. You answer, you edit, you send. It has to be yours, in your words, defensible by you in front of a client.

Why do you ask about my internet and laptop?

Because VA employers always do. Most job posts require a minimum connection speed and a backup plan for outages. Putting your real setup on your resume answers the question before it is asked, and if your setup needs an upgrade, your plan will say so honestly.

What happens to my resume and my information?

Sensitive personal details are deleted at intake, before any document is written. What remains is stored privately and used only to build your package. Nothing is posted publicly, and nothing is sent to any employer. You apply to jobs yourself.

Why is the form sometimes closed?

Intake closes whenever processing or delivery needs a safety check. That prevents new submissions from entering an uncertain system. It reopens only after the full converter path passes its verification checks.
