Your Free BFF Toolbox: How to Actually Use It

You already have a map, a quiz, a curriculum, a resume builder, a job board, and 27,000 people. The only thing missing was the order to use them in. Here it is.

Length: 25 minutes For: Filipino freelancers who just landed on BFF and feel overwhelmed Updated: 2026-06-10 (v4) Replaces: 10 Useful Skills lesson

"There is so much here. Where do I even start? Which one do I open first?"

Common question from new BFF learners

Friend, the freeze is real. You open BFF, see the Blueprint, the Archetype Finder, the Curriculum, the Resume Builder, the Job Board, and the group. So you open five tabs, read a little of each, feel overwhelmed, and close the laptop. A week later nothing has changed. That is not laziness. It is a freeze, and a freeze has a simple cure: use the tools in order.

The new rule

The BFF tools are a sequence, not a menu. At a restaurant you pick one dish and skip the rest. These tools are steps in a line: the map points to the quiz, the quiz points to the curriculum, the curriculum points to the resume builder, the resume builder points to the job board. You run them in order, once, and then the line loops as you grow.

The wrong question vs the right question

Wrong question: "Which tool is the best one to use?" This treats the tools like a menu and keeps you frozen.

Better question: "What order do I use them in?" The tools are a loop. Run them in sequence and each one hands you to the next.

Below is the whole loop. Each tool has a job and one small proof to build. Make the proof. That is how the tool becomes useful instead of just open in a tab.

One tool at a time

Do not open all six at once. Open one tool, build its small proof, then move to the next. One finished proof beats six tabs you skim and close.

Three ways people fall back into the freeze

Avoid these
  1. Hoarding tabs. Open one tool, finish its small proof, then move on. Six open tabs is the freeze wearing a disguise.
  2. Skipping the quiz. If you skip the Archetype Finder, the Curriculum and Resume Builder lose your path and you are guessing again.
  3. Applying before you have proof. Saving one matched job is calm. Spraying applications with an empty resume is the panic that burns people out.

Practice. Run the loop once, today, with your real situation.

  1. Blueprint: open it, find your box, take one screenshot.
  2. Archetype Finder: take the quiz, write one line with your archetype.
  3. My Curriculum: filter to your archetype, list your next 3 lessons, star the first.
  4. Resume Builder: pick one realistic role, generate one draft, write down one proof-project plan.
  5. Job Board: filter to your role, save one matching job, add one sentence on why it fits.

That is five small artifacts in one sitting. Not imaginary. Yours.

Where you enter the loop, based on your archetype

Everyone runs all six tools. Your archetype only changes where you lean hardest.

The Fresh StarterStarting from zero

You are not behind. The map has a place for day one, and slow is allowed.

Lean here
  1. Start at the Blueprint and go slow. Find your box first.
  2. In the Resume Builder, pick the most honest entry role, not the most impressive.
  3. Let the proof project fill a resume you thought was empty.
The Corporate TransitionerBPO or office background

Your advantage is discipline. Your gap is translating office work into remote-work proof.

Lean here
  1. Move quickly to the Resume Builder to translate your office role.
  2. Use the proof project to show async writing, not call-center scripts.
  3. Filter the Curriculum for the one skill your old job never taught.
The Creative SpecialistDesign, video, content

Your tool skill matters, but clients also need clean briefs, files, and handoffs.

Lean here
  1. Lean on the Resume Builder proof project to package your work as client-ready.
  2. Use the Curriculum to close the boring admin gap around your craft.
  3. Save Job Board listings that want a specialist, not a generalist.
The Solo EntrepreneurHas run a small business

You understand customers and money. Now show you can work inside someone else's system.

Lean here
  1. In the Resume Builder, reframe your business experience as client service.
  2. Use the Curriculum to learn one client's tool, not your own.
  3. On the Job Board, save roles where business sense is the edge.
The Generalist AdminOrganized but still broad

Your path is to pick one focus instead of staying broad and forgettable.

Lean here
  1. Use the Curriculum filter to choose one focus to go deep on.
  2. Build a Resume Builder proof project around that one focus.
  3. Save Job Board listings that match the focus, not everything.
The Polished FreelancerAlready has clients

You know the basics. Use the loop to package your process and grow.

Lean here
  1. Run the loop at a higher level: the Blueprint growth loop, not the start.
  2. Use the Resume Builder to turn a recent project into a clean case study.
  3. Use the Curriculum to add the one skill that justifies a higher rate.

Checkpoint. Show proof that you ran the loop.
