English Communications 101

"Just like any other language, English can be learned." This refresher is for building confidence, not pretending to be perfect.

Length: 25 minutes For: Anyone whose English self-rating is 3 or below Updated: 2026-05-15 (v2)

"I want to apply, but I am afraid to take client calls. I am not fluent."

Common question from new BFF learners

Friend, do not fret. Just like any other language, English can be learned. In the BPO industry, people train first before they are assigned to project-specific work. That means improvement is achievable. You can do it too. This lesson covers the basics, plus a No-Calls strategy if Zoom calls are not realistic for you yet.

The wrong question vs the right question

Wrong question: "Do I need to be 100% fluent before applying?"

Better question: "What English level is enough for my target role category, and how do I reach it?"

The idea that you must be 100% fluent is a myth. Many clients are not 100% fluent either. What they need is clear communication. Learning is 70% practice and 30% reading. Today we will walk through that framework.

The English-learning timeline for BFF mentees

Realistic English-improvement timeline

Month 1
Grammar review + pronunciation drills daily. Self-rate 30% better.
Month 2
Practice sessions weekly. Self-rate 50% better.
Month 3
First text-only client interactions. Apply to No Calls roles.
Month 6
First Zoom interview attempts. Anxiety down 70%.
Month 12
Comfortable taking calls. Apply to Customer Service or SDR roles.
The honest moment

Liz, one of the BFF success stories, rated her English 2 out of 5 when she started. She was not perfect. But she applied to text-only roles and got accepted. After three years, she became comfortable on Zoom. The only person who can limit you is yourself.

The warning about "I'll wait until I'm perfect"

The fluency paralysis

"I will learn English first, then apply" can become a three-year delay. Apply to No Calls, text-only roles while learning. Paid practice reduces fear faster than endless free YouTube study.

Other warning: do not pay anyone for "English fluency courses" that promise $10K/month income. Free resources like basicenglishspeaking.com, 7esl.com, and YouTube are enough to begin.

Practice. 30-day English challenge. Start today.

  1. Open basicenglishspeaking.com. Read 1 module.
  2. Self-rate communication skills. Speaking ___/10. Writing ___/10. Reading ___/10.
  3. Identify problem area: Writing? Speaking? Pronunciation? Pick ONE.
  4. Daily practice for 30 days: 20 minutes. YouTube pronunciation videos + 7esl.com exercises.
  5. Book a BFF practice session. DM admins via FB group.
  6. Day 30 re-rate. Improvement = data. Even +1 point matters.

Action items, based on your archetype

🌟 Polished

English likely 4-5/5. Maintenance + business vocabulary.

Do this week
  1. Watch Harvard Business Review videos for business vocab.
  2. Practice writing 1 LinkedIn post per week in English.
💼 Transitioner

BPO English is often strong in workplace conversation. The gap may be asynchronous writing.

Do this week
  1. Use Grammarly on every email.
  2. Watch documentaries with English subtitles for natural cadence.
🎨 Creative

You may be visually strong but less confident verbally. Build verbal skill through client briefs.

Do this week
  1. Practice writing 3-paragraph client briefs in English.
  2. Watch The Futur for creative-industry vocabulary.
🛒 Solo Entrepreneur

You may already have sales English. Now expand into business communication.

Do this week
  1. Translate your shop's Instagram captions into English.
  2. Practice writing investor-style emails to mock clients.
📋 Generalist

Reading/writing decent. Speaking often weaker.

Do this week
  1. Book 2 BFF practice sessions per month.
  2. Watch TED Talks at 1.25x speed for spoken cadence.
🌱 Fresh Starter

Likely lowest self-rating. Apply to No Calls roles while learning.

Do this week
  1. Start basicenglishspeaking.com daily.
  2. Apply to No Calls tab only this month.
  3. Book monthly BFF practice session.
