SMM Core Strategies

The 5 strategies of a successful Social Media Manager, turned into content pillars and a real one-week plan you build right here. WHO, WHY, WHERE, WHAT, HOW.

Length: 20 minutes For: Anyone considering or growing in SMM Updated: 2026-06-22 (v3)

"I have a client as an SMM, but I am not sure what I should post. I am just winging it."

Common question from new BFF learners

Friend, the post itself is not the problem. The problem is no foundation. When you wing it, every blank day feels like starting over. The 5 Core Strategies fix that. They turn "what do I post" into "which pillar is today," and a vague client into a clear plan. By the end of this page you will not just understand the 5 strategies. You will have a one-week posting plan written down.

The wrong question vs the right question

Wrong question: "Which viral trend should I copy today?"

Better question: "WHO is this for, WHY does it matter, WHERE are they, WHAT do they need, and HOW will I deliver it every week?"

Trends fade in a day. A foundation carries a client for a year. The 5 strategies are that foundation, and they are the same five whether the client sells lumpia or legal services.

The 5 SMM Core Strategies

Answer these five for every client. Not as slogans. As specific, written sentences.

#StrategyThe specific question to answer in writing
1WHOWho is the client, and who is the exact person we want to reach? Not "everyone." A 28-year-old mom in Manila who buys healthy snacks is a WHO. "Filipinos" is not.
2WHYWhy does this brand post at all? To sell, to teach, to build trust, to grow a community? The WHY decides the tone of every caption.
3WHEREWhere does that audience actually spend time? Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, or two of them? Post where they are, not where you like to be.
4WHATWhat does that audience need to see to know, like, and trust this brand? This becomes your content pillars, below.
5HOWHow will you deliver it every single week without burning out? This becomes your calendar and your tool workflow.

Turn WHAT into content pillars

A content pillar is a repeatable type of post. Pick 3 or 4 and you never face a blank calendar again. You rotate them. These four fit almost any brand:

PillarWhat it doesExample for a food brand
EducateTeaches something useful. Builds authority and saves."3 ways to reheat lumpia so it stays crispy."
Behind the scenesShows the people and process. Builds trust.A clip of the kitchen prepping the morning batch.
ProofCustomer reactions and results. Builds belief.A screenshot of a happy customer message.
OfferAsks for the sale. Turns attention into money."Order before 3pm for same-day delivery."
The rule of thumb

Most weeks lean educate and trust, with one clear offer. If every post is an offer, people tune out. If no post ever asks for the sale, the client does not make money and you lose the account. Balance is the skill.

Build the one-week plan

Here is the 5 strategies and 4 pillars combined into a single week. This is the artifact a client wants to see when deciding to hire you.

Example: one week for a small food brand on Instagram + Facebook

Monday
Educate. "How to keep lumpia crispy." Carousel.
Tuesday
Behind the scenes. Reel of the morning batch.
Wednesday
Proof. Repost a customer message as a graphic.
Thursday
Educate. "Which dipping sauce pairs best." Single post.
Friday
Offer. "Order before 3pm for weekend delivery." Story + post.
WHO / WHY anchor
WHO: busy Manila families. WHY: stay top of mind for weekend orders. That is why Friday is the offer.
The honest moment

Most beginners do this audit after 6 months of fumbling. That wastes half a year. Run the 5-strategy audit and write one week of pillars before you ever post. You save yourself months of "post and pray."

The warning about "I'm just posting"

The "post-and-pray" trap

Without the 5 strategies and clear pillars, SMM work becomes post-and-pray. No engagement, no growth, no results to report. The client cannot see the value, and they often disengage within 90 days. A written plan is not extra polish. It is the thing that keeps the account.

Practice. Build your own one-week plan. 25 minutes.

  1. Open a Google Doc or Sheet. Title: "SMM Plan, [brand name]."
  2. Answer the 5 strategies in one sentence each. WHO, WHY, WHERE, WHAT, HOW. Be specific on WHO.
  3. Pick 3 or 4 content pillars from the table, or name your own.
  4. Fill 5 days, one post per weekday, each tagged to a pillar. Use a real or pretend brand.
  5. Next, learn to make and schedule it. The SMM Toolkit and Workflow 2026 lesson walks the tools step by step.

Action items, based on your archetype

The Polished Freelancer~25%

SMM specialist tier. Maintain and scale.

Do this week
  1. Audit each active client against the 5 strategies. Gaps are upsell openings.
  2. Build a pillar template you reuse across clients to onboard faster.
The Corporate Transitioner~30%

Customer-facing trained. Audience empathy is a strong start.

Do this week
  1. Lead with WHO and WHERE. Your segment thinking from corporate work maps here.
  2. Build one week of pillars for a brand you would like to serve.
The Creative Specialist~15%

SMM is a creative and business hybrid. The 5 strategies are the business half.

Do this week
  1. Pair your visual skill with a written plan. Pretty plus strategy is premium.
  2. Map your portfolio pieces to pillars. Show range, not just one style.
The Solo Entrepreneur~15%

Have run your own SMM before. Translate it to client service.

Do this week
  1. Map your shop's growth to the 5 strategies. Fold your experience into the framework.
  2. Pitch "shop owner turned SMM specialist." Empathy plus strategy.
The Generalist Admin~10%

SMM is a wedge option. The 5 strategies build your structure muscle.

Do this week
  1. Build a reusable pillar and calendar template. Your admin strength is the system.
  2. Use the 5 strategies as a client intake form.
The Fresh Starter~5%

SMM is accessible but needs structure. The 5 strategies are your guard rails.

Do this week
  1. Apply the 5 strategies to your own page first. Personal IG or FB.
  2. Build a one-week plan as your first case study.
