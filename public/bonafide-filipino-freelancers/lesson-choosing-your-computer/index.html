Choosing Your Computer

"Let me tell the story first." The computer is not the goal. Keeping the client is the goal. Here is how to choose a machine that will not embarrass you on a call.

Length: 20 minutes For: Every BFF learner before Box 2 Updated: 2026-07-16 (v3, research-backed) Replaces: May 2026 (v2)

"It is on sale on Lazada. The laptop is only eight thousand pesos. Is that enough for VA work?"

The most common question in the BFF group

I was like that once. I wanted the cheapest option because I was scared of wasting money. Then I learned the lesson the hard way: a weak computer does not just slow you down, it can quietly cost you the client. The problem is not that a good machine is expensive. The problem is that a slow machine makes you wait while your client is also waiting. And a client who waits too often is a client who leaves.

So before you open Lazada, we are going to do this the right way. First we look at what the work actually demands. Then we look at what real freelancers use, from the machine you start with to the one you grow into. Then I will show you the exact problems people complain about on the forums, so you never have to learn them the hard way.

First, look at what you will actually run all day

Do not start with specs. Start with the real work. A digital marketing VA in 2026 does not open one app and close it. On a normal Tuesday, you have all of these open at the same time:

  • Google Workspace: Gmail, Docs, Sheets, and Calendar, always open
  • One project tool: Trello, Asana, or ClickUp
  • One chat tool: Slack or Microsoft Teams, pinging all day
  • Design and recording: Canva for graphics, Loom for quick videos
  • A browser with 15 to 30 tabs: client dashboards, research, schedulers, analytics
  • A live call: Zoom or Google Meet, often while everything above is still open

That is the starter load for social media and admin work. If you grow into content or video, you add Adobe Photoshop, Premiere, or CapCut, plus heavy video files.

The one idea that decides everything

Every open app and every browser tab lives inside your RAM (memory). When the RAM fills up, the computer starts using the slow disk as fake memory, and that is the exact moment everything crawls. So the question is never "can it run Canva." Almost any laptop runs Canva by itself. The real question is: "Can it run all of this at once, for eight hours, without freezing on a client call?"

The wrong question vs the right question

Wrong question: "What is the cheapest laptop I can buy?"

Right question: "What computer can keep up with my first three clients, last me three years, and still respect my family budget today?"

The first question gives you a laptop. The second gives you a career foundation. Every box in the Blueprint depends on this one.

The starter computer, and the way up

You do not need the top machine on day one. You need the right machine for the stage you are in. This is the honest ladder, from the computer you begin with to the one you grow into. The prices are July 2026 Philippine figures for reference, not fixed quotes.

🌱 Start here (Month 1) 8 GB RAM · 256 to 512 GB SSD · i5 / Ryzen 5 / Apple M1

Real example: a new Acer Aspire 5 or Lenovo IdeaPad 3 with an Intel i5, 8 GB, 256 GB SSD, around ₱28,000 to ₱40,000. The Acer Aspire 5 A515 sits near ₱35,000. A used ThinkPad in this spec can sometimes go a little lower.

Enough to carry Docs, Sheets, Gmail, one project tool, light Canva, and a video call. This can absolutely land you Client #1. Just do not stack 30 tabs on it while you are on Zoom.

💪 The real working machine 16 GB RAM · 512 GB SSD · i5+ / Ryzen 5+ / Apple M-series

Real example: MacBook Air M4 16 GB · 512 GB (~₱78,000, near 18 hours of battery) or a Lenovo Yoga Slim 7i 16 GB · 512 GB (~₱80,000).

This is the true minimum for paid digital marketing work. It runs 30 tabs plus Canva plus Slack plus Zoom, all day, without the freeze. Every serious buyer guide in 2026 lands on the same number: 16 GB is the floor for marketing multitasking.

🚀 The pro / content machine 32 GB RAM · 1 TB SSD + external SSD · M-series Pro / Intel Core Ultra

Real example: MacBook Pro M-series 32 GB, a Dell XPS 32 GB · 1 TB (~₱158,000), or a ThinkPad X1 Carbon.

For 4K video, heavy Photoshop, Premiere, and juggling large files. Adobe recommends 16 GB for Photoshop, but working editors reach for 32 GB. Only buy this once your work truly needs it.

The jump that matters

The step that changes your life is 8 GB to 16 GB, not 16 to 32. That single upgrade removes almost all of the freezing complaints you will read on the forums. If you can only afford one good decision, make it 16 GB of RAM. On Apple Silicon especially, remember you cannot add RAM later, so choose it at purchase.

Practical truth

You do not need the most expensive machine. You need one that runs the whole stack for a long stretch without giving up. The cheapest laptop on Lazada almost always fails on RAM and storage. A refurbished two-year-old machine with 16 GB of RAM will beat a brand new ₱20K laptop with 8 GB, every single time. Before you buy, you can even check the official system requirements for Canva and Photoshop and match them.

What actually breaks: the problems freelancers report

I read the freelancer forums and the Zoom and Adobe help boards so you do not have to. These are the computer problems that genuinely cost people clients. Notice something: almost none of them are about the app you use. They are about the machine underneath it.

1. "My whole computer freezes the second I join Zoom."

Why The machine is already near its limit, and the call plus the microphone driver plus your open tabs push it over. On many laptops it is also heat: the chip gets hot, then deliberately slows itself down to cool off. That is called thermal throttling.

Fix Close extra tabs before every call. Raise the laptop on a stand so the vents can breathe, and clean the fan every few months. If it still freezes on a clean, cool machine, you are simply out of RAM. That is your signal to move up the ladder.

2. "It gets slower every single month."

Why Your SSD is filling up. Once storage passes about 85% full, the system has no room for temporary files, so everything drags.

Fix Keep 15 to 20% of the drive free at all times. Move finished client files to an external SSD or the cloud, do not hoard them on the laptop.

3. "Canva or Photoshop lags and the tools delay."

Why These are memory hungry. Adobe itself recommends 16 GB for Photoshop, and busy files want more. On 8 GB, filters and brushes stutter.

Fix 16 GB is the real floor for design work. Until you upgrade, close other apps and turn on Canva's Memory Saver.

4. "The client says I keep cutting out on calls."

Why This one is almost always the internet, not the computer. A 16 GB laptop on 5 Mbps still fails.

Fix See the universal rule below. Test your line on Speedtest.net and keep a mobile hotspot as backup.

5. "My laptop broke and I lost a week of income."

Why One machine, no backup. When it dies, so does your work and your pay for that week.

Fix The moment you have two clients, buy a cheap second machine. A refurbished Chromebook is enough. A repair should never mean zero pesos.

Philippine reality: the brownout problem

An old or weak battery plus a power cut equals a dropped client call with no warning. Test your real battery life in the practice below, and keep a small UPS or a charged power bank that can run your router. In the Philippines, this is not optional, it is part of the job.

This lesson is not from memory. It is built from the 2026 digital marketing buyer guides, the official Canva and Adobe Photoshop requirements, the Zoom community lag threads, and the Wise Philippines VA laptop guide. Every number above traces back to one of these.

An honest setup, with honest math

Not every "perfect setup" photo online is real. Most working freelancers have limits too. Here is what a solid, honest setup actually looks like.

A common solid daily driver

A mid-tier MacBook Air or a Windows equivalent with Apple M-series, Intel i5+, or AMD Ryzen 5+

Memory
16 GB for serious work · 32 GB if budget allows and you touch video
Storage
512 GB SSD target · 1 TB if you store client video or design files
Keep it fresh
Update the OS for security and client-app compatibility
Watch the memory hogs
Chrome with 30+ tabs · Notion · video editors · multiple chat apps

A smart pattern for tight budgets: pair a portable laptop with an older but upgradeable desktop. Many older Windows desktops and Intel iMacs let you swap in more RAM cheaply. Older hardware with upgradeable memory often outlasts a brand new laptop with soldered 8 GB.

The consequence we do not talk about enough

If you buy a laptop with too little RAM just because it was cheap, this is what can happen: you accept an interview, you join Zoom, the computer freezes, the audio cuts out. That night the client says, "let's go with someone else, their setup is more stable." Your skill did not fail. Your tools did. If you save ₱5,000 today but lose a ₱30,000 per month client because of it, that is not savings. That is expensive economy.

Practice. Do this now, not tomorrow.

Open the computer you are using right now and run this 6-step audit. It takes about 12 minutes.

  1. Check RAM. Mac: Apple menu, About This Mac. Windows: Settings, System, About. Write down how much you have.
  2. Check storage. Mac: About This Mac, Storage. Windows: Settings, Storage. How much free space, and is it under 15%?
  3. Check the chip. Same screen. May "Intel Core i3" ba? "Apple M1"? "Ryzen 5"?
  4. Time your battery. Unplug the charger and browse OnlineJobs.ph. If it drops 50% in under 2 hours, the battery is already weak.
  5. Test the call. Open Google Meet to yourself. Does the mic work? The webcam? Are you too dark because the room light is poor?
  6. Stress it. Open Chrome with 15 tabs plus Canva plus your video call, all at once. Does it stay smooth, or does it start to stutter? That is your honest ceiling.

The audit form. Write this on paper, yes, on paper:

  • My RAM: ____ GB
  • My storage: ____ GB free of ____ GB total
  • My processor: ____
  • My battery: lasted ____ minutes unplugged
  • My webcam: works / blurry / broken
  • My mic: clear / hissy / broken
  • Under full load it stayed: smooth / stuttered / froze

Your move, based on your archetype

Our journeys are not the same. A Polished Freelancer starts from a different baseline than a Fresh Starter. The VA Resume Converter has a quiz that routes you to your archetype. If you do not know yours yet, take that quiz first, then come back here. Here is the action plan for this lesson, by archetype.

🌟 The Polished Freelancer ~25% · proceed ~80%

Already running your own client roster. Multi-platform. Talks pricing without flinching.

Do this week
  1. Audit against your client load. With 3+ active clients, 16 GB should be your minimum today. If you are still on 8 GB, plan the upgrade inside 6 months.
  2. Add a backup machine. A refurbished Chromebook or older iMac from Swappa as your failover, so a broken laptop never means lost income.
  3. Pre-claim the upgrade. Put it in your budget sheet: "Q4 2026, 32 GB MacBook, paid from retainer #3." Make it a business expense, not personal spending.
Recommended pick: Apple Certified Refurbished 14" MacBook Pro M-series, 16 GB or 32 GB, 1 TB. Pay with client money and record it in your books.
💼 The Corporate Transitioner ~30% · proceed ~60%

BPO English plus office discipline. Strong from years of phone work. Needs the freelancing bridge.

Do this week
  1. Do not quit your job to buy a computer. Finish your notice period first. Use your 13th month pay plus leave conversion to fund the machine.
  2. Go mid-range. A 16 GB Lenovo Yoga Slim 7i or a refurbished ThinkPad, around ₱35,000 to ₱80,000, will last you four years.
  3. Verify home internet first. Run Speedtest. If you are under 25 Mbps, get a backup mobile hotspot before you spend on the laptop.
Recommended pick: Lenovo Yoga Slim 7i or ThinkPad T-series 16 GB · 512 GB, or a MacBook Air M4 16 GB if budget allows.
🎨 The Creative Specialist ~15% · proceed ~70%

Photoshop, Premiere, video editing. Portfolio is your wedge. Admin tooling thin.

Do this week
  1. Your machine must run Adobe Creative Cloud and CapCut without choking. 16 GB is the floor, not the target. Choose 32 GB if you do 4K video. 8 GB is a no.
  2. External SSD, non-negotiable. Add a SanDisk Extreme Portable SSD 1 TB (~₱5,500). Keep project files there, not on internal storage.
  3. Mind your colors. If you do photo or video, calibrate through DisplayCAL or check your display color profile. Wrong colors mean unprofessional renders.
Recommended pick: MacBook Pro M-series 16 GB · 512 GB, or a refurbished 32 GB unit from OWC for 4K work.
🛒 The Solo Entrepreneur ~15% · proceed ~55%

Ran your own ecom or store. Self-taught Canva, Shopify, Mailchimp. Often a mom with a full schedule.

Do this week
  1. Treat it as a business expense. Keep the receipt for your tax filing. The laptop is depreciable equipment, not personal spending.
  2. Pick something the kids cannot break. Do not leave a MacBook Air on the coffee table. Store it in a cabinet when not in use. A Chromebook is the sturdy budget option.
  3. Load your tools day one. Install Google Calendar, Trello, and Toggl Track on the first day. Use the business skill you already have.
Recommended pick: Acer Chromebook Spin 8 GB (~₱20,000 to ₱25,000) for kid-proof daily use, or an ASUS VivoBook 16 GB if budget supports.
📋 The Generalist Admin ~10% · proceed ~50%

Excel-fluent, calendar-disciplined. No specialty wedge yet.

Do this week
  1. Match the machine to a wedge. A bookkeeping VA needs QuickBooks compatibility (Windows is easier). An inbox VA needs a reliable webcam and mic. Pick the wedge before the laptop.
  2. Mid-range Windows is fine. You do not need a MacBook. A Dell Inspiron 16 GB · 512 GB covers 95% of admin VA work.
  3. Add a second monitor. An ₱8K monitor from Shopee is a productivity multiplier: client email on the left, your work on the right.
Recommended pick: Dell Inspiron 15 16 GB · 512 GB i5 (~₱40,000 to ₱50,000), or a refurbished ThinkPad E-series.
🌱 The Fresh Starter ~5% · proceed ~30%

Eager but unprepared. 0 to 2 tools. Weak English self-rating. Confidence gap is the bottleneck.

Do this week
  1. You do not need to buy right away. Before spending ₱30K, finish Blueprint stages 1 and 2. Buy once you are committed to the skill.
  2. Borrow first if you can. Borrow a laptop from a sister or cousin for your first 30 days. Test whether you truly want this. Land a first client on OnlineJobs.ph, then use that first paycheck for the upgrade.
  3. If you must buy, use 0% installment. Do not drain your savings. Check Home Credit or Lazada 0% for 6 months. Let your first 3 payments fund it.
Recommended pick: an Acer Aspire 5 i5 · 8 GB · 256 GB, around ₱28,000 to ₱35,000, for the first 12 months. A used unit can be cheaper if the budget is very tight, just check the chip is really an i5. Upgrade to a 16 GB machine after Client #2.
Universal rule for everyone

For every archetype: internet speed and a backup plan matter as much as the laptop. A large share of new freelancers sit below 10 Mbps. If you have a 16 GB MacBook but only 5 Mbps internet, Zoom will still fail. Test on Speedtest.net, keep a mobile hotspot as backup, and put your result on your resume.
