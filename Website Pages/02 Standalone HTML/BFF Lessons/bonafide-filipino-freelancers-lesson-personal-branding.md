Personal Branding

<!-- wbl-text:0015 -->
"Personal Branding is what people tell about you when you are not in the room."

<!-- wbl-text:0016 -->
Length:

<!-- wbl-text:0017 -->
25 minutes

<!-- wbl-text:0018 -->
For:

<!-- wbl-text:0019 -->
Every freelancer ready to position themselves intentionally

<!-- wbl-text:0020 -->
Updated:

<!-- wbl-text:0021 -->
2026-05-15 (v2)

<!-- wbl-text:0022 -->
Replaces:

<!-- wbl-text:0023 -->
2019 lesson

<!-- wbl-text:0024 -->
"What is personal branding really? Is it only for influencers?"

<!-- wbl-text:0025 -->
Common question from new BFF learners

<!-- wbl-text:0026 -->
It is not only for influencers. Friend, whether you like it or not,

<!-- wbl-text:0027 -->
you already have a personal brand.

<!-- wbl-text:0028 -->
The question is whether you shape it intentionally or leave it random. Your reputation, what clients say about you when you are not in the room, that is your brand. You can shape it on purpose instead of leaving it to luck.

<!-- wbl-text:0029 -->
The wrong question vs the right question

<!-- wbl-text:0030 -->
Wrong question:

<!-- wbl-text:0031 -->
"What logo and colors should I make for my personal brand?"

<!-- wbl-text:0032 -->
Better question:

<!-- wbl-text:0033 -->
"What specific story, message, and value do I want associated with my name?"

<!-- wbl-text:0034 -->
Logo is third order. First order is

<!-- wbl-text:0035 -->
story + message + personality + culture.

<!-- wbl-text:0036 -->
Those are the 4 elements of personal branding we will discuss today. All of them are free to develop, with nothing you need to pay for.

<!-- wbl-text:0037 -->
The 4 elements of personal branding

<!-- wbl-text:0038 -->
Element

<!-- wbl-text:0039 -->
What it is

<!-- wbl-text:0040 -->
Question to answer

<!-- wbl-text:0041 -->
1. Branding Identity / UVP

<!-- wbl-text:0042 -->
Your unique value proposition. What makes you specifically irreplaceable. Your story.

<!-- wbl-text:0043 -->
"What's my story? What's unique about me?"

<!-- wbl-text:0044 -->
2. Branding Message

<!-- wbl-text:0045 -->
The one consistent thing you say. The truth you repeat. Not what you sell, but what you stand for.

<!-- wbl-text:0046 -->
"What's the truth I want everyone to remember?"

<!-- wbl-text:0047 -->
3. Branding Personality

<!-- wbl-text:0048 -->
How you speak. Your tone. Your voice. How you engage with people.

<!-- wbl-text:0049 -->
"What kind of professional am I?"

<!-- wbl-text:0050 -->
4. Branding Culture

<!-- wbl-text:0051 -->
The community you build. The standards you keep. The values that bond the team.

<!-- wbl-text:0052 -->
"What environment do I create around me?"

<!-- wbl-text:0053 -->
The reframe

<!-- wbl-text:0054 -->
All the elements are

<!-- wbl-text:0055 -->
extracted from real experience

<!-- wbl-text:0056 -->
. Not fabricated. Not "what should I say?" but "what is true about me that is also valuable to others?"

<!-- wbl-text:0057 -->
Example: a 4-element brand filled in

<!-- wbl-text:0058 -->
Here's a template, with example phrasings for each element. Replace with your own real material.

<!-- wbl-text:0059 -->
Example 4-element brand

<!-- wbl-text:0060 -->
1. Identity / UVP

<!-- wbl-text:0061 -->
Example pattern: A single sentence that names what you've survived, learned, or built, and the value you bring because of it.

<!-- wbl-text:0062 -->
2. Message

<!-- wbl-text:0063 -->
Example pattern: The ONE truth you want every reader to remember. 1-2 sentences max.

<!-- wbl-text:0064 -->
3. Personality

<!-- wbl-text:0065 -->
Example pattern: How you show up. "Warm peer, not guru. Free knowledge, not gated courses." Tone words that describe your voice.

<!-- wbl-text:0066 -->
4. Culture

<!-- wbl-text:0067 -->
Example pattern: Who is welcome, how you work together, and the holistic context (business + skill + relationship + personal dev + spiritual).

<!-- wbl-text:0068 -->
The honest moment

<!-- wbl-text:0069 -->
The strongest part of any identity statement is usually the part you'd rather not say out loud, the hard parts of your story.

<!-- wbl-text:0070 -->
That is what makes it true. And that is why your voice has credibility.

<!-- wbl-text:0071 -->
The part of your story that feels uncomfortable may be your differentiator.

<!-- wbl-text:0072 -->
The warning about "fake brand syndrome"

<!-- wbl-text:0073 -->
The Filipino crab mentality risk

<!-- wbl-text:0074 -->
If you pretend on social media to be a "luxury lifestyle freelancer" while in reality you are still working hard for your first ₱20K, that is

<!-- wbl-text:0075 -->
fake brand syndrome

<!-- wbl-text:0076 -->
. When the client sees the gap between online and reality, the trust deficit grows.

<!-- wbl-text:0077 -->
Build honestly, not aspirationally.

<!-- wbl-text:0078 -->
Other warning:

<!-- wbl-text:0079 -->
kindness is the best tool that people rarely use. Pulling others down will not help.

<!-- wbl-text:0080 -->
"Look at me, others are wrong" branding can be popular, but it is short-term. "Kindness + competence" grows slower, but it is sustainable.

<!-- wbl-text:0081 -->
Practice. Write your 4-element brand. 30 minutes.

<!-- wbl-text:0082 -->
Open

<!-- wbl-text:0083 -->
Google Doc

<!-- wbl-text:0084 -->
.

<!-- wbl-text:0085 -->
Title: "My Personal Brand v1."

<!-- wbl-text:0086 -->
Identity / UVP (10 minutes):

<!-- wbl-text:0087 -->
Write your story. What's unique? What did you survive, learn, or build? Not the resume bullets. The real story.

<!-- wbl-text:0088 -->
Message (5 minutes):

<!-- wbl-text:0089 -->
Write the ONE truth you want everyone to remember. 1-2 sentences max.

<!-- wbl-text:0090 -->
Personality (5 minutes):

<!-- wbl-text:0091 -->
Write 3 adjectives. Then write what you're NOT. ("Warm, honest, practical. NOT salesy, NOT preachy, NOT generic.")

<!-- wbl-text:0092 -->
Culture (5 minutes):

<!-- wbl-text:0093 -->
Write the values you operate by. What do you celebrate? What do you refuse?

<!-- wbl-text:0094 -->
Test it:

<!-- wbl-text:0095 -->
Send the doc to 2 BFF friends. Ask: "Does this sound like me?" If yes, ship. If no, iterate.

Action items, based on your archetype

🌟 The Polished Freelancer

<!-- wbl-text:0098 -->
~25% · proceed ~80%

<!-- wbl-text:0099 -->
Brand exists implicitly. Now formalize and amplify.

<!-- wbl-text:0100 -->
Do this week

<!-- wbl-text:0101 -->
Audit your

<!-- wbl-text:0102 -->
LinkedIn

<!-- wbl-text:0103 -->
, IG, portfolio.

<!-- wbl-text:0104 -->
Are the 4 elements consistent across surfaces?

<!-- wbl-text:0105 -->
Build a "case studies" section on your portfolio.

<!-- wbl-text:0106 -->
The 4 elements need PROOF, not just claims.

<!-- wbl-text:0107 -->
Pick 1 thought-leadership platform.

<!-- wbl-text:0108 -->
Weekly LinkedIn post or biweekly blog. Brand by repetition.

<!-- wbl-text:0109 -->
💼 The Corporate Transitioner

<!-- wbl-text:0110 -->
~30% · proceed ~60%

<!-- wbl-text:0111 -->
Often "BPO trained" is the unintentional brand. Reshape intentionally.

<!-- wbl-text:0112 -->
Do this week

<!-- wbl-text:0113 -->
Reframe BPO years as advantage.

<!-- wbl-text:0114 -->
"X years of customer empathy, escalation management, SLA discipline." Not generic "BPO."

<!-- wbl-text:0115 -->
Surface night-shift availability as deliberate.

<!-- wbl-text:0116 -->
"Available US business hours; my mornings are family time."

<!-- wbl-text:0117 -->
Build a "How I Translate BPO Skills to Remote VA Work" LinkedIn post.

<!-- wbl-text:0118 -->
Show meta-skill.

<!-- wbl-text:0119 -->
🎨 The Creative Specialist

<!-- wbl-text:0120 -->
~15% · proceed ~70%

<!-- wbl-text:0121 -->
Visual brand natural. Verbal + business brand often missing.

<!-- wbl-text:0122 -->
Do this week

<!-- wbl-text:0123 -->
Pair visual brand with brand voice.

<!-- wbl-text:0124 -->
Captions, bios, About sections: verbalize the visual.

<!-- wbl-text:0125 -->
Pick a niche specialty + own it.

<!-- wbl-text:0126 -->
"F

<!-- wbl-text:0127 -->
Instagram Specialist" beats "Designer for Hire."

<!-- wbl-text:0128 -->
Document your creative process.

<!-- wbl-text:0129 -->
Behind-the-scenes Reels or process posts. "How I work" stands out.

<!-- wbl-text:0130 -->
🛒 The Solo Entrepreneur

<!-- wbl-text:0131 -->
~15% · proceed ~55%

<!-- wbl-text:0132 -->
Entrepreneur background is differentiator. Position around it.

<!-- wbl-text:0133 -->
Do this week

<!-- wbl-text:0134 -->
Brand as "VA who's been on the client side."

<!-- wbl-text:0135 -->
Empathy + ops know-how = senior signal.

<!-- wbl-text:0136 -->
Share business lessons on LinkedIn.

<!-- wbl-text:0137 -->
"What running a shop taught me about [VA topic]" stands out.

<!-- wbl-text:0138 -->
Niche to e-commerce VAs.

<!-- wbl-text:0139 -->
"Shopify Operations VA for SMEs" is specific + monetizable.

<!-- wbl-text:0140 -->
📋 The Generalist Admin

<!-- wbl-text:0141 -->
~10% · proceed ~50%

<!-- wbl-text:0142 -->
Risk: generic brand. Pick wedge + commit.

<!-- wbl-text:0143 -->
Do this week

<!-- wbl-text:0144 -->
One wedge = one brand.

<!-- wbl-text:0145 -->
"Bookkeeping VA" or "Calendar EA." Not both.

<!-- wbl-text:0146 -->
Make the wedge your IG bio + LinkedIn headline.

<!-- wbl-text:0147 -->
Repetition = brand.

<!-- wbl-text:0148 -->
Build a "how I [wedge]" content series.

<!-- wbl-text:0149 -->
10 posts demonstrating expertise.

<!-- wbl-text:0150 -->
🌱 The Fresh Starter

<!-- wbl-text:0151 -->
~5% · proceed ~30%

<!-- wbl-text:0152 -->
No brand yet. Start with story, not skill.

<!-- wbl-text:0153 -->
Do this week

<!-- wbl-text:0154 -->
Lead with WHY.

<!-- wbl-text:0155 -->
"Why I'm becoming a VA" story. Aspirations + reasons. Not credentials.

<!-- wbl-text:0156 -->
Document the learning journey.

<!-- wbl-text:0157 -->
"Week 1 of BFF: what I learned." Brand by transparency.

<!-- wbl-text:0158 -->
Don't try to look senior.

<!-- wbl-text:0159 -->
The "fresh learner" brand can be authentic + appealing. Lean into it.

<!-- wbl-text:0160 -->
The application pack consistency check

<!-- wbl-text:0161 -->
Run this before you send anything

<!-- wbl-text:0162 -->
Your profile summary, your LinkedIn headline, and your pitch message get read together. They must agree. A 30-second check on every application:

<!-- wbl-text:0163 -->
Same tool list everywhere.

<!-- wbl-text:0164 -->
If your profile names Canva, Trello, and Slack, your pitch names the same three. A tool that shows up in one place but not another looks like a mistake.

<!-- wbl-text:0165 -->
Vary your verbs.

<!-- wbl-text:0166 -->
Do not start every line with "Handled" or "Managed." Repetition reads like a template, not a person.

<!-- wbl-text:0167 -->
Kill the boilerplate.

<!-- wbl-text:0168 -->
If your "I do not overclaim" line is word-for-word identical on every application, it stops meaning anything. Make each one specific to the role.

<!-- wbl-text:0169 -->
One name, one headline, one promise.

<!-- wbl-text:0170 -->
The lane you claim in your bio is the same lane you pitch.
