Interviews (Async + Video)

<!-- wbl-text:0015 -->
The two interview formats modern VA candidates face are async text interviews and live video interviews. Each format needs a different preparation pattern.

<!-- wbl-text:0016 -->
Length:

<!-- wbl-text:0017 -->
25 minutes

<!-- wbl-text:0018 -->
For:

<!-- wbl-text:0019 -->
VAs preparing for first round of interviews

<!-- wbl-text:0020 -->
Updated:

<!-- wbl-text:0021 -->
2026-05-15 (v1)

<!-- wbl-text:0022 -->
Prerequisite:

<!-- wbl-text:0023 -->
Personal Branding · Resume Builder

<!-- wbl-text:0024 -->
"The client sent me a Google Form with 8 questions. I do not know what I should write."

<!-- wbl-text:0025 -->
Common question from new BFF learners

<!-- wbl-text:0026 -->
An async text interview is

<!-- wbl-text:0027 -->
not lesser than a video interview

<!-- wbl-text:0028 -->
. It is the format chosen by many modern VA hires. The skill is different from video interviewing. Clarity, structure, and brevity are the metrics. Video interviews measure presence, spoken language, and reasoning aloud. These are two different muscles. This lesson covers both.

<!-- wbl-text:0029 -->
The wrong question vs the right question

<!-- wbl-text:0030 -->
Wrong question:

<!-- wbl-text:0031 -->
"What should I say in the interview?"

<!-- wbl-text:0032 -->
Better question:

<!-- wbl-text:0033 -->
"What 3 things does the client need to know about me, and what 3 things do I need to confirm about them?"

<!-- wbl-text:0034 -->
An interview is a

<!-- wbl-text:0035 -->
two-way information exchange

<!-- wbl-text:0036 -->
, not a quiz. The client wants to verify whether you can do the work, show up reliably, and communicate clearly. You want to verify whether the pay is fair, the client is respectful, and the work is scoped clearly. Both sides need to leave with enough information to make a decision.

<!-- wbl-text:0037 -->
The 5 differences: async-text vs video interview

<!-- wbl-text:0038 -->
Dimension

<!-- wbl-text:0039 -->
Async-text (Google Form, Loom, Slack DM)

<!-- wbl-text:0040 -->
Video (Zoom, Meet, in-platform)

<!-- wbl-text:0041 -->
Time format

<!-- wbl-text:0042 -->
Take your time. Edit. Re-read. 4-24 hours window typical.

<!-- wbl-text:0043 -->
Real-time. 30-60 min slot. No edit button.

<!-- wbl-text:0044 -->
Measured skill

<!-- wbl-text:0045 -->
Written clarity + structure + thoughtfulness

<!-- wbl-text:0046 -->
Spoken English + presence + reasoning aloud

<!-- wbl-text:0047 -->
Cheat sheet

<!-- wbl-text:0048 -->
Notes encouraged. STAR examples ready. Drafts welcomed.

<!-- wbl-text:0049 -->
Subtle notes okay. No reading off script.

<!-- wbl-text:0050 -->
Setup

<!-- wbl-text:0051 -->
Quiet desk + clear thinking. Distractions tolerable.

<!-- wbl-text:0052 -->
Lit, clean background, working mic, headphones. No distractions.

<!-- wbl-text:0053 -->
Decision speed

<!-- wbl-text:0054 -->
Slower. Hiring manager reviews batches. 3-7 days response typical.

<!-- wbl-text:0055 -->
Faster. Decision often within 24-48 hours.

<!-- wbl-text:0056 -->
Industry shift toward async

<!-- wbl-text:0057 -->
2026 trend: companies prefer async interviews for first-round screening. Reasons: timezone friendly, more inclusive for shy or accent-anxious candidates, and easier to review with a hiring committee. Live video is increasingly reserved for final rounds only. Async text interviewing is

<!-- wbl-text:0058 -->
increasingly the door

<!-- wbl-text:0059 -->
.

<!-- wbl-text:0060 -->
Example: a common solid async-text answer (the STAR pattern)

<!-- wbl-text:0061 -->
Question: "Tell me about a time you handled a difficult customer."

<!-- wbl-text:0062 -->
Situation (1-2 sentences)

<!-- wbl-text:0063 -->
"In my previous role at a [type of business], a returning customer disputed a charge they had authorized 3 weeks earlier."

<!-- wbl-text:0064 -->
Task (1 sentence)

<!-- wbl-text:0065 -->
"My responsibility was to resolve the dispute without escalating to refund-by-default."

<!-- wbl-text:0066 -->
Action (3-4 sentences)

<!-- wbl-text:0067 -->
"I first acknowledged the customer's frustration in writing. I then pulled the order history and emailed them the signed acknowledgment they had given at purchase. I offered two options: a partial refund or a discount on their next order."

<!-- wbl-text:0068 -->
Result (1-2 sentences)

<!-- wbl-text:0069 -->
"They chose the discount on next order, used it within 2 weeks, and became a repeat buyer. The case closed without escalation or chargeback."

<!-- wbl-text:0070 -->
Total word count

<!-- wbl-text:0071 -->
~120 words. Concrete numbers, time markers, specific actions. Beats vague "I handled the situation calmly" answer 10x.

<!-- wbl-text:0072 -->
If you have no past role

<!-- wbl-text:0073 -->
Use a BFF learning anecdote: "I had to teach myself X in 2 weeks because [reason]. I broke it into 3 steps. Here is the outcome." Same STAR shape, learning-based content.

<!-- wbl-text:0074 -->
The architecture insight

<!-- wbl-text:0075 -->
STAR is not a script.

<!-- wbl-text:0076 -->
It is the structure.

<!-- wbl-text:0077 -->
If you prepare STAR answers for 5 common questions, difficult customer, missed deadline, learning a new tool, conflict with a colleague, and biggest achievement, you can handle most interview questions. The remaining questions are usually scenario twists. The structure is portable.

<!-- wbl-text:0078 -->
The warnings people do not talk about

<!-- wbl-text:0079 -->
Do not paste AI-generated answers verbatim

<!-- wbl-text:0080 -->
Hiring managers in 2026 are skilled at detecting ChatGPT-shaped answers. The tells: word counts are too uniform, vocabulary is too polished, there are no specific names or dates, and every example sounds "innovative" or "elevated."

<!-- wbl-text:0081 -->
Use AI as a draft starter, not the final answer.

<!-- wbl-text:0082 -->
Edit aggressively, add specifics from your real experience, and replace flat phrasing with your voice.

<!-- wbl-text:0083 -->
Do not pretend to know what you don't

<!-- wbl-text:0084 -->
"Have you used HubSpot?" "Yes, briefly." If you haven't, say so.

<!-- wbl-text:0085 -->
Better answer:

<!-- wbl-text:0086 -->
"Not yet, but I have used Pipedrive which has similar pipeline logic. I expect 1-2 weeks ramp-up." Honest + concrete + transferable. Pretending will surface in onboarding and end the contract.

<!-- wbl-text:0087 -->
Practice. 25 minutes, two formats, real run.

<!-- wbl-text:0088 -->
Prepare 5 STAR-ed answers in writing.

<!-- wbl-text:0089 -->
Topics: difficult customer, missed deadline, learning a new tool, conflict, biggest achievement. ~120 words each. Save in a Google Doc.

<!-- wbl-text:0090 -->
Async-text simulation (10 min).

<!-- wbl-text:0091 -->
Pretend client sent you a Google Form with 3 questions. Set a 30-minute timer. Write your answers as if for real. Re-read once, edit once, submit (close the doc).

<!-- wbl-text:0092 -->
Video setup test (5 min).

<!-- wbl-text:0093 -->
Open Zoom or

<!-- wbl-text:0094 -->
Google Meet

<!-- wbl-text:0095 -->
. Join as yourself. Check: face lit from front, plain background, mic clear, eyes at camera level (not pointing up at face).

<!-- wbl-text:0096 -->
Video answer practice (10 min).

<!-- wbl-text:0097 -->
Record yourself answering one STAR question aloud. Watch playback. Did you use "umm" too much? Did you stay under 90 seconds? Did you look at camera or screen?

<!-- wbl-text:0098 -->
Prepare your 3 questions to ask the interviewer.

<!-- wbl-text:0099 -->
Topic ideas: "What does success look like in this role 90 days in?" "What is the team's current biggest priority?" "What does communication cadence look like (Slack, weekly call, async updates)?" Specific beats generic.

<!-- wbl-text:0100 -->
Audit checklist:

<!-- wbl-text:0101 -->
5 STAR-ed answers written in Google Doc

<!-- wbl-text:0102 -->
Async-text simulation completed under time pressure

<!-- wbl-text:0103 -->
Video setup verified via self-test

<!-- wbl-text:0104 -->
Video answer recorded and reviewed

<!-- wbl-text:0105 -->
3 interviewer questions prepared

<!-- wbl-text:0106 -->
Tone audit: warm + clear + specific in writing, calm + paced on video

<!-- wbl-text:0107 -->
Action items by archetype

<!-- wbl-text:0108 -->
The Polished Freelancer

<!-- wbl-text:0109 -->
~25% · pre-built portfolio shortcuts interview

<!-- wbl-text:0110 -->
You already have a portfolio. In the interview, your job is to confirm fit, not prove basic competence. That requires a different posture.

<!-- wbl-text:0111 -->
Do this week

<!-- wbl-text:0112 -->
Build a 1-page "case study deck."

<!-- wbl-text:0113 -->
Use 3 past clients in problem-action-result format. Sending this before the video call can shorten the interview because the proof is already visible.

<!-- wbl-text:0114 -->
Practice the "diagnosis" framing.

<!-- wbl-text:0115 -->
Ask: "Where does this team currently feel stuck?" A diagnostic posture sounds more senior.

<!-- wbl-text:0116 -->
Bring your rate confidently.

<!-- wbl-text:0117 -->
"My current retainer band is $X-Y. Where does this role fit?" Direct, not apologetic.

<!-- wbl-text:0118 -->
Recommended posture:

<!-- wbl-text:0119 -->
Peer-to-peer conversation. Not "please hire me." More "let's see if this is a fit for both of us."

<!-- wbl-text:0120 -->
The Corporate Transitioner

<!-- wbl-text:0121 -->
~30% · async = your strength

<!-- wbl-text:0122 -->
A BPO background usually means you are comfortable on calls. But the async text format may be unfamiliar. Practice writing under time pressure.

<!-- wbl-text:0123 -->
Do this week

<!-- wbl-text:0124 -->
Translate BPO metrics to freelance language in STAR answers.

<!-- wbl-text:0125 -->
"AHT 4:30" becomes "average resolution under 5 min." "Quality 92" becomes "92% quality score on supervisor audits."

<!-- wbl-text:0126 -->
Practice async-text under timer.

<!-- wbl-text:0127 -->
8 questions × 5 min each = 40 min total budget for typical Google Form interview.

<!-- wbl-text:0128 -->
Lean on your tenure.

<!-- wbl-text:0129 -->
"I have done X for 4 years across Y volume" is a real advantage over fresh starters. Name the numbers.

<!-- wbl-text:0130 -->
Recommended posture:

<!-- wbl-text:0131 -->
Experienced-but-transitioning. Honest about new format, confident in transferable skills.

<!-- wbl-text:0132 -->
The Creative Specialist

<!-- wbl-text:0133 -->
~15% · portfolio > resume

<!-- wbl-text:0134 -->
Your visual portfolio may carry most of the interview decision. Prepare the link, the share settings, and the screen-share flow.

<!-- wbl-text:0135 -->
Do this week

<!-- wbl-text:0136 -->
Portfolio link in your application.

<!-- wbl-text:0137 -->
Behance

<!-- wbl-text:0138 -->
,

<!-- wbl-text:0139 -->
Dribbble

<!-- wbl-text:0140 -->
, Vimeo, or your own site. Top 5 pieces, top of the page.

<!-- wbl-text:0141 -->
Prepare 3 case study walkthroughs (2 min each).

<!-- wbl-text:0142 -->
For video: "Here's the brief, here's my approach, here's the result." Practice screen-share + voiceover.

<!-- wbl-text:0143 -->
Be ready for "design test" requests.

<!-- wbl-text:0144 -->
Some clients send a 1-day mini-brief. Decide your rule: paid for tests >2 hours, free for ≤1 hour. Set the policy.

<!-- wbl-text:0145 -->
Recommended posture:

<!-- wbl-text:0146 -->
Portfolio-led. Less talking, more showing. Walk through real work.

<!-- wbl-text:0147 -->
The Solo Entrepreneur

<!-- wbl-text:0148 -->
~15% · business-owner perspective

<!-- wbl-text:0149 -->
Your shop or business experience is your differentiator. Frame it as business intuition, not beginner VA energy.

<!-- wbl-text:0150 -->
Do this week

<!-- wbl-text:0151 -->
Prepare 3 business-owner STAR answers.

<!-- wbl-text:0152 -->
Inventory crisis, customer dispute, vendor problem. Real, concrete, with numbers.

<!-- wbl-text:0153 -->
Frame transition as wedge, not pivot.

<!-- wbl-text:0154 -->
"I am still running [shop] while adding VA work because the skills overlap. Owners trust other owners."

<!-- wbl-text:0155 -->
Lean into ecom-client opportunities.

<!-- wbl-text:0156 -->
E-commerce interviewers value candidates who can say, "I've been on the other side of this conversation."

<!-- wbl-text:0157 -->
Recommended posture:

<!-- wbl-text:0158 -->
Business owner adding a service line. Not "fresh VA looking for first job."

<!-- wbl-text:0159 -->
The Generalist Admin

<!-- wbl-text:0160 -->
~10% · systematic prep advantage

<!-- wbl-text:0161 -->
Admin discipline is your strength in the interview. Prepared, organized, and calm is the goal.

<!-- wbl-text:0162 -->
Do this week

<!-- wbl-text:0163 -->
Build an interview tracker in Trello or Sheets.

<!-- wbl-text:0164 -->
Columns: company, role, applied date, interview date, format, my notes, status. The tracker shows your organization habit.

<!-- wbl-text:0165 -->
Practice the "I have a question" close.

<!-- wbl-text:0166 -->
"Before we wrap, can I confirm the next 3 steps?" That close sounds senior.

<!-- wbl-text:0167 -->
Lean into specificity.

<!-- wbl-text:0168 -->
Numbers, dates, tools, processes. Concrete beats abstract every time.

<!-- wbl-text:0169 -->
Recommended posture:

<!-- wbl-text:0170 -->
Process-strong, calm, prepared. Less "selling," more "structured conversation."

<!-- wbl-text:0171 -->
The Fresh Starter

<!-- wbl-text:0172 -->
~5% · honesty + enthusiasm

<!-- wbl-text:0173 -->
If you do not have a past role for STAR answers, use BFF learning anecdotes, family or personal scenarios, and clear willingness to learn.

<!-- wbl-text:0174 -->
Do this week

<!-- wbl-text:0175 -->
Prepare 5 STAR-ed answers using BFF + family + personal scenarios.

<!-- wbl-text:0176 -->
Example: "I had to learn Trello in 1 week to organize BFF Job Hunt. Here's what I did, here's the result."

<!-- wbl-text:0177 -->
Practice the "I am willing to grow" closing.

<!-- wbl-text:0178 -->
"I am willing to take entry rate for the first 90 days while I build retention. After that, let's revisit." Frames willingness without desperation.

<!-- wbl-text:0179 -->
Practice video call setup 3 times before real interview.

<!-- wbl-text:0180 -->
Familiarity with Zoom + mic + lighting reduces interview-day anxiety.

<!-- wbl-text:0181 -->
Recommended posture:

<!-- wbl-text:0182 -->
Eager + honest about freshness + ready to learn. Avoid faking experience.

<!-- wbl-text:0183 -->
Universal rule

<!-- wbl-text:0184 -->
For every archetype:

<!-- wbl-text:0185 -->
treat the interview as the start of the work, not a separate event.

<!-- wbl-text:0186 -->
Your pacing, clarity, and professionalism in the interview preview your daily work. If you are warm, clear, and specific in the interview, that is what the client expects 90 days in. This is not pretending. It is your natural working style, polished.
