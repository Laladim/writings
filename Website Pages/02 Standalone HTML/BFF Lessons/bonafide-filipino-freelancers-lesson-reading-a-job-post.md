Reading a Job Post: Red Flags and Scam Filters

<!-- wbl-text:0015 -->
It is one in the morning. The pay looks high, the work looks easy, it says start today. Here is what to do in the next two minutes after your heart jumps.

<!-- wbl-text:0016 -->
Length:

<!-- wbl-text:0017 -->
25 minutes

<!-- wbl-text:0018 -->
For:

<!-- wbl-text:0019 -->
Anyone applying to remote jobs, especially first-timers

<!-- wbl-text:0020 -->
Updated:

<!-- wbl-text:0021 -->
2026-06-08 (v1)

<!-- wbl-text:0022 -->
Prerequisite:

<!-- wbl-text:0023 -->
Choosing My Client

<!-- wbl-text:0024 -->
"The post looked perfect. High pay, easy work, start today. I just wanted to say yes before someone else got it."

<!-- wbl-text:0025 -->
A feeling we hear often from new applicants

<!-- wbl-text:0026 -->
That jump in your chest is normal. The post looks perfect: high pay, easy work, start today. Before you reply, pause for two minutes. This lesson gives you one scan for the moment when hope is loud and your judgment is tired. It protects your money, samples, IDs, and energy before a job post asks too much from you.

<!-- wbl-text:0027 -->
The wrong question vs the right question

<!-- wbl-text:0028 -->
Wrong question:

<!-- wbl-text:0029 -->
"Is this client a good person?"

<!-- wbl-text:0030 -->
Better question:

<!-- wbl-text:0031 -->
"Which risk model fits this post before I spend money, samples, IDs, or emotional energy?"

<!-- wbl-text:0032 -->
You are not judging whether they are good people. You cannot know that from a post. You are checking

<!-- wbl-text:0033 -->
what this post is asking you to risk

<!-- wbl-text:0034 -->
. That shift, from judging character to checking risk, is the whole mindset. It removes the guilt of saying no, and it works even when you are tired and hopeful at 1am.

<!-- wbl-text:0035 -->
The 7-Signal Scan

<!-- wbl-text:0036 -->
This scan exists because the pattern kept repeating. I read 641 applicant records and sorted more than 150 platforms by the problems that kept showing up. Warning signs appeared about

<!-- wbl-text:0037 -->
twice as often in the rejected pile, 22% against 11%

<!-- wbl-text:0038 -->
. Red flags are not rare, but you do not need to memorize every scam. You need

<!-- wbl-text:0039 -->
seven repeating signals

<!-- wbl-text:0040 -->
.

<!-- wbl-text:0041 -->
Signal

<!-- wbl-text:0042 -->
What it looks like

<!-- wbl-text:0043 -->
The tell

<!-- wbl-text:0044 -->
1. Pay-to-apply

<!-- wbl-text:0045 -->
Registration fee, deposit, "buy your own equipment first," or "pay for our training, then you qualify."

<!-- wbl-text:0046 -->
Money flows from you, not to you

<!-- wbl-text:0047 -->
2. Spec work

<!-- wbl-text:0048 -->
An unpaid "test" that is actually their real, finished deliverable. A full article, a full edit, a complete design.

<!-- wbl-text:0049 -->
Test over an hour, or publishable as-is

<!-- wbl-text:0050 -->
3. Ghost job

<!-- wbl-text:0051 -->
Vague scope, no real company, the same post reappears for months, exists to collect resumes or samples.

<!-- wbl-text:0052 -->
You cannot tell what the job is

<!-- wbl-text:0053 -->
4. Lowball for senior work

<!-- wbl-text:0054 -->
Run our whole social media, manage our team, own our strategy, paid like a simple data entry task.

<!-- wbl-text:0055 -->
Big scope plus tiny pay

<!-- wbl-text:0056 -->
5. Opaque vetting

<!-- wbl-text:0057 -->
Will not name the company, no real interview, wants you to commit before you know anything.

<!-- wbl-text:0058 -->
They hide the basics

<!-- wbl-text:0059 -->
6. Payment-protection gap

<!-- wbl-text:0060 -->
Pushed off-platform with no record, no contract, crypto or gift cards, or "we overpaid, send the balance back."

<!-- wbl-text:0061 -->
Off the record, no contract

<!-- wbl-text:0062 -->
7. Support black hole

<!-- wbl-text:0063 -->
Only contact is a personal Gmail, WhatsApp, or Facebook with no company behind it. Or a real brand name used by a fake sender.

<!-- wbl-text:0064 -->
Check the sender, not the name

<!-- wbl-text:0065 -->
Normal platform fees are not pay-to-apply

<!-- wbl-text:0066 -->
Real platforms make money by taking a small cut from what you

<!-- wbl-text:0067 -->
earn

<!-- wbl-text:0068 -->
, after you earn it. A freelancer service fee on a platform is normal. A job asking

<!-- wbl-text:0069 -->
you

<!-- wbl-text:0070 -->
to pay before you start is the anomaly. The direction of the money is the whole test.

<!-- wbl-text:0071 -->
The scan in action, on a real-looking post

<!-- wbl-text:0072 -->
Here is a post. I made it up, but it is built from shapes seen many times. No real company, no real names.

<!-- wbl-text:0073 -->
Example post (invented)

<!-- wbl-text:0074 -->
URGENT: Hiring Social Media Manager + Admin. Very easy work, high pay, start today. To apply, pay a small processing fee and message us on WhatsApp. We may overpay your first week by mistake, just send the balance back.

<!-- wbl-text:0075 -->
Pay a processing fee

<!-- wbl-text:0076 -->
Signal 1, pay-to-apply. Instant.

<!-- wbl-text:0077 -->
Message us on WhatsApp

<!-- wbl-text:0078 -->
Signal 7, support black hole. No company behind it.

<!-- wbl-text:0079 -->
Overpay and send back

<!-- wbl-text:0080 -->
Signal 6, payment-protection gap. Always a scam.

<!-- wbl-text:0081 -->
Two big roles, easy-work pay

<!-- wbl-text:0082 -->
Signal 4, lowball for senior work.

<!-- wbl-text:0083 -->
No company name anywhere

<!-- wbl-text:0084 -->
Signal 5, opaque vetting.

<!-- wbl-text:0085 -->
Count

<!-- wbl-text:0086 -->
Five of seven signals in one short post. Reject, no reply needed.

<!-- wbl-text:0087 -->
One signal vs a cluster

<!-- wbl-text:0088 -->
One signal alone is not always a scam. A vague post might just be a busy founder who wrote a lazy ad, and that is worth

<!-- wbl-text:0089 -->
one question

<!-- wbl-text:0090 -->
. But a

<!-- wbl-text:0091 -->
cluster, three or more signals together, is a clear reject

<!-- wbl-text:0092 -->
. You do not need to investigate. You do not owe them a reply. Move on and keep your energy.

<!-- wbl-text:0093 -->
After the scan: Reject, Clarify, or Apply

<!-- wbl-text:0094 -->
Decision

<!-- wbl-text:0095 -->
When

<!-- wbl-text:0096 -->
What you do

<!-- wbl-text:0097 -->
Reject now

<!-- wbl-text:0098 -->
Pay-to-apply, off-platform payment with no contract, the overpayment-refund trick, or a fake sender using a real brand.

<!-- wbl-text:0099 -->
No reply needed. Close it. Move on.

<!-- wbl-text:0100 -->
Clarify first

<!-- wbl-text:0101 -->
Unclear but not obviously a trap: vague scope, a large test, no company name yet.

<!-- wbl-text:0102 -->
Ask one specific question. The answer decides.

<!-- wbl-text:0103 -->
Apply

<!-- wbl-text:0104 -->
Zero or one soft signal, clear scope, a company you can find, a normal process.

<!-- wbl-text:0105 -->
Send a strong, specific application. Go for it.

<!-- wbl-text:0106 -->
Two clarifying questions that work

<!-- wbl-text:0107 -->
"Can you share the company name and what the first month looks like?" or "Is the test paid, and how long should it take?" A real client answers plainly. A trap gets annoyed, vague, or disappears. The response is the real test.

<!-- wbl-text:0108 -->
The warnings people usually skip

<!-- wbl-text:0109 -->
Not every flag is a scam

<!-- wbl-text:0110 -->
Some clients are just messy. A disorganized post might be a tired founder, not a criminal. That is exactly why

<!-- wbl-text:0111 -->
Clarify

<!-- wbl-text:0112 -->
exists. The scan protects your money, samples, IDs, and energy. It does not need to protect your pride. Stay calm, just verify before you spend.

<!-- wbl-text:0113 -->
Verify the sender, not just the name

<!-- wbl-text:0114 -->
A famous company name in a message means nothing if the reply comes from a random free email. Check the address. Check that the company actually has the role posted on their own real page. Two minutes of checking the sender beats a very convincing fake.

<!-- wbl-text:0115 -->
A question is professional, not rude

<!-- wbl-text:0116 -->
Every good client respects a thoughtful question. The ones who get angry that you asked are the ones you are glad to lose. Your questions are a filter that works in both directions.

<!-- wbl-text:0117 -->
Practice. Two minutes, one real post, the full scan.

<!-- wbl-text:0118 -->
Find one real job post

<!-- wbl-text:0119 -->
you are actually considering right now. Open it.

<!-- wbl-text:0120 -->
Run the seven signals, line by line.

<!-- wbl-text:0121 -->
Pay-to-apply, spec work, ghost job, lowball for senior work, opaque vetting, payment-protection gap, support black hole.

<!-- wbl-text:0122 -->
Count how many fire.

<!-- wbl-text:0123 -->
Zero or one soft signal is usually fine. Three or more is a reject.

<!-- wbl-text:0124 -->
Land on a verdict:

<!-- wbl-text:0125 -->
reject, clarify, or apply.

<!-- wbl-text:0126 -->
If you chose clarify,

<!-- wbl-text:0127 -->
write the one specific question you would send.

<!-- wbl-text:0128 -->
If you chose apply,

<!-- wbl-text:0129 -->
confirm the company is findable and the process is normal, then send a strong application.

<!-- wbl-text:0130 -->
Audit checklist:

<!-- wbl-text:0131 -->
One real post selected

<!-- wbl-text:0132 -->
All 7 signals checked against it

<!-- wbl-text:0133 -->
Signal count written down

<!-- wbl-text:0134 -->
Verdict chosen: reject / clarify / apply

<!-- wbl-text:0135 -->
Sender address verified (if applying)

<!-- wbl-text:0136 -->
One clarifying question drafted (if clarifying)

<!-- wbl-text:0137 -->
Action items, based on your archetype

<!-- wbl-text:0138 -->
🌱 The Fresh Starter

<!-- wbl-text:0139 -->
most at risk · scan every post

<!-- wbl-text:0140 -->
You are the prime target for free-sample harvesting and pay-to-apply traps because you are hungry for the first yes. The scan is your training wheels until your instincts catch up.

<!-- wbl-text:0141 -->
Do this week

<!-- wbl-text:0142 -->
Screenshot the 7-Signal card

<!-- wbl-text:0143 -->
and keep it open beside you while you apply. Run it on every post, no exceptions.

<!-- wbl-text:0144 -->
Never send a free sample over an hour.

<!-- wbl-text:0145 -->
Use one sample you already made instead. Build practice samples in the Google Sites Portfolio lesson so you always have one ready.

<!-- wbl-text:0146 -->
Practice on five posts this week,

<!-- wbl-text:0147 -->
even ones you will not apply to. Counting signals out loud trains the habit fast.

<!-- wbl-text:0148 -->
Recommended habit:

<!-- wbl-text:0149 -->
See, scan, count, decide. Two minutes before every reply.

<!-- wbl-text:0150 -->
🌟 The Polished Freelancer

<!-- wbl-text:0151 -->
volume risk · protect your time

<!-- wbl-text:0152 -->
Your risk is not naivety, it is volume. Good posts and bad posts blur when you scan fast. The cluster rule keeps you efficient.

<!-- wbl-text:0153 -->
Do this week

<!-- wbl-text:0154 -->
Use the cluster rule ruthlessly.

<!-- wbl-text:0155 -->
Three or more signals is an instant pass. Do not spend energy investigating bad posts.

<!-- wbl-text:0156 -->
Pre-write your two clarifying questions

<!-- wbl-text:0157 -->
so middle-box posts take seconds, not minutes.

<!-- wbl-text:0158 -->
Watch lowball-for-senior closely.

<!-- wbl-text:0159 -->
At your level, the trap is big scope at assistant pay dressed up as "exciting."

<!-- wbl-text:0160 -->
Recommended habit:

<!-- wbl-text:0161 -->
Fast scan, hard cluster cutoff, reusable questions.

<!-- wbl-text:0162 -->
💼 The Corporate Transitioner

<!-- wbl-text:0163 -->
process-savvy · trust the process shape

<!-- wbl-text:0164 -->
You know what a real hiring process looks like from your office years. Use that. Opaque vetting will feel wrong to you faster than to most.

<!-- wbl-text:0165 -->
Do this week

<!-- wbl-text:0166 -->
Compare each post to a real hiring process.

<!-- wbl-text:0167 -->
Clear role, clear company, a next step. If those are missing, that is signal 5.

<!-- wbl-text:0168 -->
Do not assume remote means informal.

<!-- wbl-text:0169 -->
A legitimate remote client still names themselves and pays on the record.

<!-- wbl-text:0170 -->
Ask the company-name question early.

<!-- wbl-text:0171 -->
You are comfortable asking; use it as your default clarifier.

<!-- wbl-text:0172 -->
Recommended habit:

<!-- wbl-text:0173 -->
Match every post against a normal process shape.

<!-- wbl-text:0174 -->
🎨 The Creative Specialist

<!-- wbl-text:0175 -->
spec-work target · guard your samples

<!-- wbl-text:0176 -->
Spec work targets you most. "Just do this one design so we can see your skill" is how finished creative work gets taken for free.

<!-- wbl-text:0177 -->
Do this week

<!-- wbl-text:0178 -->
Lead with an existing portfolio piece,

<!-- wbl-text:0179 -->
never a fresh free deliverable. Your samples are already proof.

<!-- wbl-text:0180 -->
Set the test boundary:

<!-- wbl-text:0181 -->
a short timed task is fine, a full publishable asset is not.

<!-- wbl-text:0182 -->
Watch for "contest" posts

<!-- wbl-text:0183 -->
that collect many finished designs and pay none of them.

<!-- wbl-text:0184 -->
Recommended habit:

<!-- wbl-text:0185 -->
Show existing work, refuse free deliverables.

<!-- wbl-text:0186 -->
🛒 The Solo Entrepreneur

<!-- wbl-text:0187 -->
time-scarce · your time is the cost

<!-- wbl-text:0188 -->
You already run a business, so your scarce resource is hours. A ghost job or a long unpaid test steals the time you owe your own shop.

<!-- wbl-text:0189 -->
Do this week

<!-- wbl-text:0190 -->
Reject ghost jobs fast.

<!-- wbl-text:0191 -->
If you cannot tell what the work actually is, it is not worth your limited hours.

<!-- wbl-text:0192 -->
Treat your time like inventory.

<!-- wbl-text:0193 -->
An unpaid test over an hour is stock you are giving away.

<!-- wbl-text:0194 -->
Keep payment on the record,

<!-- wbl-text:0195 -->
the same way you would protect a supplier transaction.

<!-- wbl-text:0196 -->
Recommended habit:

<!-- wbl-text:0197 -->
Protect hours like inventory, reject the vague.

<!-- wbl-text:0198 -->
📋 The Generalist Admin

<!-- wbl-text:0199 -->
systematizer · make the scan a system

<!-- wbl-text:0200 -->
You are organized by nature. Turn the scan into a saved checklist and you will out-filter most applicants without slowing down.

<!-- wbl-text:0201 -->
Do this week

<!-- wbl-text:0202 -->
Build the 7-Signal scan into a saved note or board

<!-- wbl-text:0203 -->
you tick through for each post.

<!-- wbl-text:0204 -->
Log your verdicts.

<!-- wbl-text:0205 -->
Over a month you will see which signals show up most in your niche.

<!-- wbl-text:0206 -->
Pair this with Choosing My Client

<!-- wbl-text:0207 -->
so fit and safety are one combined filter.

<!-- wbl-text:0208 -->
Recommended habit:

<!-- wbl-text:0209 -->
A saved checklist plus a verdict log.

<!-- wbl-text:0210 -->
Universal rule

<!-- wbl-text:0211 -->
For every archetype:

<!-- wbl-text:0212 -->
the scan protects your money, samples, IDs, and energy, not your pride.

<!-- wbl-text:0213 -->
Most posts are fine and go straight to Apply. The scan is not there to make you afraid of everything. It is there so that when you do say yes, you say it to the right ones.
