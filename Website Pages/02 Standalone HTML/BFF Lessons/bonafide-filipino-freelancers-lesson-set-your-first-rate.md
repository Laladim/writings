Pricing: Set Your First Rate

<!-- wbl-text:0015 -->
A client asks your rate. You type a number, get scared, and delete it for a smaller one. Here is how to set a floor you never go below, and quote it without it feeling like begging.

<!-- wbl-text:0016 -->
Length:

<!-- wbl-text:0017 -->
25 minutes

<!-- wbl-text:0018 -->
For:

<!-- wbl-text:0019 -->
Anyone about to quote a client for the first time

<!-- wbl-text:0020 -->
Updated:

<!-- wbl-text:0021 -->
2026-06-08 (v1)

<!-- wbl-text:0022 -->
Prerequisite:

<!-- wbl-text:0023 -->
Common Mistakes

<!-- wbl-text:0024 -->
"I typed my rate, stared at it, then deleted it and typed something lower. By the time I hit send, the job was barely worth my time."

<!-- wbl-text:0025 -->
A feeling we hear often from new freelancers

<!-- wbl-text:0026 -->
That shrinking number is the first thing a client ever learns about how you value your own work. A low rate feels safe, but it is not. It attracts the clients who do not value the work, who ask for endless changes, and who leave the moment someone cheaper appears. This lesson gives you two things:

<!-- wbl-text:0027 -->
a floor you never go below

<!-- wbl-text:0028 -->
, and

<!-- wbl-text:0029 -->
a simple script

<!-- wbl-text:0030 -->
to quote it out loud without begging.

<!-- wbl-text:0031 -->
The wrong question vs the right question

<!-- wbl-text:0032 -->
Wrong question:

<!-- wbl-text:0033 -->
"What is the lowest rate that might get me hired?"

<!-- wbl-text:0034 -->
Better question:

<!-- wbl-text:0035 -->
"What is the value I give a client abroad, and what floor protects my time?"

<!-- wbl-text:0036 -->
You are not applying for a local job that pays local wages. You are offering a service to a client abroad who pays in dollars.

<!-- wbl-text:0037 -->
You earn in dollars. You stay in the Philippines.

<!-- wbl-text:0038 -->
So stop pricing yourself against the job down the street. Price the value you give a client across the world.

<!-- wbl-text:0039 -->
The Rate Floor Method

<!-- wbl-text:0040 -->
Five steps. You will leave with a floor written down and a quote ready to send.

<!-- wbl-text:0041 -->
Step

<!-- wbl-text:0042 -->
What you do

<!-- wbl-text:0043 -->
Why it works

<!-- wbl-text:0044 -->
1. Find your floor

<!-- wbl-text:0045 -->
Monthly need divided by realistic billable hours equals your minimum hourly. Write it down privately.

<!-- wbl-text:0046 -->
A line you never cross

<!-- wbl-text:0047 -->
2. Price in dollars

<!-- wbl-text:0048 -->
Think and quote in your client's currency, not your local one.

<!-- wbl-text:0049 -->
Stops you anchoring low

<!-- wbl-text:0050 -->
3. Specialist beats generalist

<!-- wbl-text:0051 -->
Lead with one clear promise instead of a long skill list. Keep the other skills, just lead with one.

<!-- wbl-text:0052 -->
Roughly 3x the pay for a clear lane

<!-- wbl-text:0053 -->
4. Quote a range

<!-- wbl-text:0054 -->
Name a range, not a single number. The bottom stays above your floor; the top is where you would love to land.

<!-- wbl-text:0055 -->
Starts a conversation, not a dead end

<!-- wbl-text:0056 -->
5. Protect the floor

<!-- wbl-text:0057 -->
When a client pushes back, adjust the scope, never the floor. Below your floor, the honest answer is "not the right fit."

<!-- wbl-text:0058 -->
Walking away protects the business

<!-- wbl-text:0059 -->
Why a specialist earns more

<!-- wbl-text:0060 -->
In our community data, the pay gap between a general helper and a focused specialist is roughly

<!-- wbl-text:0061 -->
three times

<!-- wbl-text:0062 -->
, for picking a lane. Not because the specialist works harder, but because a clear, specific skill is easier to trust and easier to charge for. Being the person who does one thing well beats being the person who does everything cheaply.

<!-- wbl-text:0063 -->
The four-part quoting script

<!-- wbl-text:0064 -->
Knowing your number is one thing. Saying it out loud without your voice shaking is another. Fill in these four parts and you have a complete, confident quote, with no apology and no nervous over-explaining.

<!-- wbl-text:0065 -->
Fill in the four parts

<!-- wbl-text:0066 -->
1. What you do

<!-- wbl-text:0067 -->
One clear line, led by your specialty. "I help busy founders keep their inbox and calendar under control."

<!-- wbl-text:0068 -->
2. The outcome

<!-- wbl-text:0069 -->
What the client gets. "So they get their mornings back and never miss an important message."

<!-- wbl-text:0070 -->
3. Your range

<!-- wbl-text:0071 -->
Said plainly. "For a setup like this, my rate usually falls in this range."

<!-- wbl-text:0072 -->
4. The next step

<!-- wbl-text:0073 -->
One easy action. "If that works for you, I can send a short plan this week."

<!-- wbl-text:0074 -->
Saying the number out loud: pause, state, stop

<!-- wbl-text:0075 -->
When you say your range, do three things.

<!-- wbl-text:0076 -->
Pause. State the range. Then stop talking.

<!-- wbl-text:0077 -->
Most people ruin a good quote by nervously adding "but I can go lower" before the client even answers. Do not fill the silence. The silence is the client thinking, which is exactly what you want.

<!-- wbl-text:0078 -->
The method in action, a first quote

<!-- wbl-text:0079 -->
An example person, invented, not a real client. A new social media assistant gets her first real inquiry. Her instinct is to quote low. Instead she uses the method.

<!-- wbl-text:0080 -->
Scared quote vs clear quote

<!-- wbl-text:0081 -->
The scared quote

<!-- wbl-text:0082 -->
"Hi, um, I can do this for a really low number, but I am flexible, I can go lower if needed, whatever works for you."

<!-- wbl-text:0083 -->
The clear quote

<!-- wbl-text:0084 -->
"I help wellness coaches post consistently without the daily stress. You get a full content calendar and your time back. For a setup like this, my rate usually falls in this range. If that works, I can send a short plan this week."

<!-- wbl-text:0085 -->
What changed:

<!-- wbl-text:0086 -->
she found her floor privately, sharpened "I do social media" into one clear promise, set a range with the bottom above her floor, and sent it without shrinking a single number. Same skills she had an hour ago.

<!-- wbl-text:0087 -->
The warnings people usually skip

<!-- wbl-text:0088 -->
Do not undercut to compete

<!-- wbl-text:0089 -->
There is always someone willing to go lower. The lowest price is

<!-- wbl-text:0090 -->
a race you win by losing

<!-- wbl-text:0091 -->
. The client who only cares about the cheapest hands will leave you for the next cheaper person anyway. Compete on clarity and trust, never on price.

<!-- wbl-text:0092 -->
Do not bury yourself in free extras

<!-- wbl-text:0093 -->
"I will do this, and also this, and also that, all for the tiny price" is just underpricing with more steps. It exhausts you and trains the client to expect everything for nothing.

<!-- wbl-text:0094 -->
More work is a reason to charge more, never a reason to charge less.

<!-- wbl-text:0095 -->
Charge clients well, never pay to work

<!-- wbl-text:0096 -->
You should never pay to learn how to earn, and you should never pay to work either. No expensive course promising secret jobs, no fee to join a magic client list. You charge for your work. Your money flows one direction: toward you.

<!-- wbl-text:0097 -->
Practice. Floor, range, quote. Today.

Action items, based on your archetype

<!-- wbl-text:0118 -->
🌱 The Fresh Starter

<!-- wbl-text:0119 -->
most likely to underprice

<!-- wbl-text:0120 -->
You feel you have not earned the right to charge, so you shrink the number. The floor removes the guessing and the guilt.

<!-- wbl-text:0121 -->
Do this week

<!-- wbl-text:0122 -->
Calculate your floor before any client asks.

<!-- wbl-text:0123 -->
Decide the line now, while you are calm, not mid-conversation when you are nervous.

<!-- wbl-text:0124 -->
Practice the four-part quote out loud five times.

<!-- wbl-text:0125 -->
The words feel natural only after you have heard your own voice say them.

<!-- wbl-text:0126 -->
Never trade your floor for "experience."

<!-- wbl-text:0127 -->
Build proof in the Google Sites Portfolio lesson instead of in unpaid client work.

<!-- wbl-text:0128 -->
Recommended habit:

<!-- wbl-text:0129 -->
Floor first, range above it, pause-state-stop.

<!-- wbl-text:0130 -->
🌟 The Polished Freelancer

<!-- wbl-text:0131 -->
leave money on the table

<!-- wbl-text:0132 -->
Your risk is not nerves, it is habit. You may be charging an old rate that no longer matches your skill or your specialty.

<!-- wbl-text:0133 -->
Do this week

<!-- wbl-text:0134 -->
Recompute your floor with current numbers.

<!-- wbl-text:0135 -->
Your costs and your skill have both grown; your floor probably has too.

<!-- wbl-text:0136 -->
Lead with your strongest specialty,

<!-- wbl-text:0137 -->
not a general menu. The clearer lane carries the higher range.

<!-- wbl-text:0138 -->
Quote ranges, not fixed numbers,

<!-- wbl-text:0139 -->
so larger-scope clients can place themselves at your top.

<!-- wbl-text:0140 -->
Recommended habit:

<!-- wbl-text:0141 -->
Refresh the floor, lead with the sharpest lane.

<!-- wbl-text:0142 -->
💼 The Corporate Transitioner

<!-- wbl-text:0143 -->
anchor to a local salary

<!-- wbl-text:0144 -->
Your trap is anchoring your rate to your old office salary. Remote client work abroad is a different market with a different number.

<!-- wbl-text:0145 -->
Do this week

<!-- wbl-text:0146 -->
Drop the salary anchor.

<!-- wbl-text:0147 -->
Think in your client's currency, not your last payslip.

<!-- wbl-text:0148 -->
Translate your office expertise into one clear promise

<!-- wbl-text:0149 -->
the client recognizes, then price the specialist range.

<!-- wbl-text:0150 -->
Use your comfort with professionalism

<!-- wbl-text:0151 -->
to deliver the quote calmly, pause and all.

<!-- wbl-text:0152 -->
Recommended habit:

<!-- wbl-text:0153 -->
Client-currency thinking, specialist framing.

<!-- wbl-text:0154 -->
🎨 The Creative Specialist

<!-- wbl-text:0155 -->
undervalue finished work

<!-- wbl-text:0156 -->
Creatives often price by hours instead of by the value of a finished asset. A great video is worth more than the time it took.

<!-- wbl-text:0157 -->
Do this week

<!-- wbl-text:0158 -->
Price the outcome, not the hours.

<!-- wbl-text:0159 -->
The result the client gets is the value, not your editing time.

<!-- wbl-text:0160 -->
Lead with a niche

<!-- wbl-text:0161 -->
(a content type, an industry, a platform) to claim the specialist range.

<!-- wbl-text:0162 -->
Quote a range per project

<!-- wbl-text:0163 -->
so bigger briefs land at your top, not your floor.

<!-- wbl-text:0164 -->
Recommended habit:

<!-- wbl-text:0165 -->
Value-based, niche-led, range per project.

<!-- wbl-text:0166 -->
🛒 The Solo Entrepreneur

<!-- wbl-text:0167 -->
price like a favor

<!-- wbl-text:0168 -->
You already understand margins from your own business. Apply that same discipline to your freelance rate.

<!-- wbl-text:0169 -->
Do this week

<!-- wbl-text:0170 -->
Treat your time as a cost line.

<!-- wbl-text:0171 -->
A rate below your floor runs your service at a loss, the same as selling below cost.

<!-- wbl-text:0172 -->
Lead with the operations niche

<!-- wbl-text:0173 -->
you know best from running your own shop.

<!-- wbl-text:0174 -->
Hold the floor under pushback

<!-- wbl-text:0175 -->
the way you would refuse a supplier deal that loses money.

<!-- wbl-text:0176 -->
Recommended habit:

<!-- wbl-text:0177 -->
Margin thinking, ops niche, hold the floor.

<!-- wbl-text:0178 -->
📋 The Generalist Admin

<!-- wbl-text:0179 -->
stuck at generalist pay

<!-- wbl-text:0180 -->
Doing everything is exactly what keeps your rate low. The 3x specialist gap is your biggest opportunity.

<!-- wbl-text:0181 -->
Do this week

<!-- wbl-text:0182 -->
Pick one lane to lead with

<!-- wbl-text:0183 -->
(inbox, calendar, data, CRM) even while you still offer the rest.

<!-- wbl-text:0184 -->
Set a floor that assumes the specialist rate,

<!-- wbl-text:0185 -->
not the generalist one you are used to.

<!-- wbl-text:0186 -->
Rewrite your pitch

<!-- wbl-text:0187 -->
from "I do everything" to "I help X do Y." Same work, higher range.

<!-- wbl-text:0188 -->
Recommended habit:

<!-- wbl-text:0189 -->
Lead with one lane, price as a specialist.

<!-- wbl-text:0190 -->
Universal rule

<!-- wbl-text:0191 -->
For every archetype:

<!-- wbl-text:0192 -->
your first rate is the anchor for everything after.

<!-- wbl-text:0193 -->
It is hard to raise a price with a client who hired you cheap, and much easier to start fair and grow. Build the first number on a floor, not on fear.
