"SDR roles pay well, but I am not a salesperson by nature. Can I apply?"

<!-- wbl-text:0025 -->
Common question from new SWAHG learners

<!-- wbl-text:0026 -->
Yes. Modern SDR work is

<!-- wbl-text:0027 -->
highly templated, highly process-driven

<!-- wbl-text:0028 -->
. The old "smile and dial" stereotype is obsolete in 2026. Today's SDR work is 80% async writing + research + tool fluency, and 20% calls. Many Filipino VAs thrive in this role, especially in "non-calls SDR" or "BDR (Business Development Rep)" variants. The skill is templated. The lesson is the template.

<!-- wbl-text:0029 -->
The wrong question vs the right question

<!-- wbl-text:0030 -->
Wrong question:

<!-- wbl-text:0031 -->
"How do I become good at sales?"

<!-- wbl-text:0032 -->
Better question:

<!-- wbl-text:0033 -->
"What 4-step cadence + 2 qualification frameworks + 3 tools do I use to become a predictable SDR?"

<!-- wbl-text:0034 -->
The 4 components:

<!-- wbl-text:0035 -->
(1) Cadence design, (2) Qualification framework (BANT or MEDDIC), (3) Tool stack (Apollo + Outreach/Lemlist + CRM), (4) Deliverability hygiene

<!-- wbl-text:0036 -->
. Master these 4, and within 30 days you can work as an SDR.

<!-- wbl-text:0037 -->
The 4 components of a modern SDR system

<!-- wbl-text:0038 -->
Component

<!-- wbl-text:0039 -->
What it does

<!-- wbl-text:0040 -->
VA daily activity

<!-- wbl-text:0041 -->
1. Cadence

<!-- wbl-text:0042 -->
Sequence of touchpoints across days. Example: Day 1 email, Day 3 LinkedIn connect, Day 6 follow-up email, Day 10 LinkedIn message, Day 14 final email. 5-7 touches over 14-21 days.

<!-- wbl-text:0043 -->
Run 30-50 contacts/day

<!-- wbl-text:0044 -->
2. Qualification framework

<!-- wbl-text:0045 -->
BANT: Budget, Authority, Need, Timeline. MEDDIC: Metrics, Economic buyer, Decision criteria, Decision process, Identify pain, Champion. Used to score replies.

<!-- wbl-text:0046 -->
Score every response

<!-- wbl-text:0047 -->
3. Tool stack

<!-- wbl-text:0048 -->
Apollo or Lemlist for prospecting + outreach. Salesforce/HubSpot for tracking. LinkedIn Sales Nav for research. This trio runs modern outbound.

<!-- wbl-text:0049 -->
Use all 3 daily

<!-- wbl-text:0050 -->
4. Deliverability

<!-- wbl-text:0051 -->
Domain warmup, SPF/DKIM/DMARC, sending volume limits, spam-word avoidance, unsubscribe handling. The silent killer of campaigns.

<!-- wbl-text:0052 -->
Monitor weekly

<!-- wbl-text:0053 -->
Industry benchmark numbers (2026)

<!-- wbl-text:0054 -->
Cold email reply rate:

<!-- wbl-text:0055 -->
3-8% for well-targeted lists.

<!-- wbl-text:0056 -->
LinkedIn connect acceptance:

<!-- wbl-text:0057 -->
25-40%.

<!-- wbl-text:0058 -->
Qualified meetings booked per 100 contacts:

<!-- wbl-text:0059 -->
2-5. If you are below these numbers, there may be an issue with list quality, message, or deliverability. Not just volume.

<!-- wbl-text:0082 -->
Example: a common solid 5-touch cold email cadence

<!-- wbl-text:0083 -->
14-day cadence targeting a mid-market SaaS founder

<!-- wbl-text:0084 -->
Day 1 · Email 1 · The hook

<!-- wbl-text:0085 -->
50-70 words. One specific personal observation about their company (recent funding, hiring, blog post). One question. No pitch. "Hey [name], saw [specific event]. Curious how you're thinking about [related pain]? Open to a quick exchange."

<!-- wbl-text:0086 -->
Day 3 · LinkedIn connect

<!-- wbl-text:0087 -->
Connection request with 1 sentence note. Same topic as email but different surface. "Loved your post about X. Connecting to follow your work."

<!-- wbl-text:0088 -->
Day 6 · Email 2 · Value drop

<!-- wbl-text:0089 -->
50 words. Share something useful unrelated to your pitch: an article, a tool, an insight. "Saw this and thought of your team's [X focus]: [link]. Not pitching anything, thought you'd find useful."

<!-- wbl-text:0090 -->
Day 10 · LinkedIn message

<!-- wbl-text:0091 -->
If connection accepted: short message in DM. "Hey [name], are you open to chatting briefly about [topic]? Happy to share what I've seen across other [their industry] teams."

<!-- wbl-text:0092 -->
Day 14 · Email 3 · The breakup

<!-- wbl-text:0093 -->
30 words. "Closing the loop. If [topic] becomes a priority again, I'm here. Otherwise wishing you well." Honest, not aggressive. Sometimes triggers reply.

<!-- wbl-text:0094 -->
Across all 5 touches

<!-- wbl-text:0095 -->
Same target person + same topic thread + different angle each time. Total time investment: ~30 minutes to build initial cadence, ~5 min per contact to personalize.

<!-- wbl-text:0096 -->
The architecture insight

<!-- wbl-text:0097 -->
Cold outreach is

<!-- wbl-text:0098 -->
not about being clever

<!-- wbl-text:0099 -->
. It is about

<!-- wbl-text:0100 -->
being relevant + brief + persistent without being annoying

<!-- wbl-text:0101 -->
. A short message under 70 words that shows you did research, with a specific question, beats the 300-word "value-prop dump" 5-to-1 in reply rate.

<!-- wbl-text:0102 -->
The warnings people usually skip

<!-- wbl-text:0103 -->
Spam filters are aggressive in 2026

<!-- wbl-text:0104 -->
Cold email deliverability has tightened. Gmail + Outlook 2024-2026 updates penalize: high send volume from new domains, generic content, no unsubscribe link, suspicious links.

<!-- wbl-text:0105 -->
Use a separate sending domain

<!-- wbl-text:0106 -->
(e.g., client.com → outreach.client.com), warm it up for 2 weeks before campaigns, cap sends at 30-50/day per inbox. Not the 500/day blast mentality.

<!-- wbl-text:0107 -->
Do not lie in cold outreach

<!-- wbl-text:0108 -->
The temptation to inflate: "10x'd their revenue," "worked with 50+ clients," "featured in Forbes." In SDR work, lies get caught fast. Client checks LinkedIn, asks for case study, asks for the Forbes link. Career-ending. Stay honest: "I've helped [N] teams in [industry] reach [specific outcome]" with real numbers, even if small.

Build a target persona.

<!-- wbl-text:0114 -->

<!-- wbl-text:0115 -->
Pull a list of 25 contacts

<!-- wbl-text:0116 -->

<!-- wbl-text:0117 -->
Draft your 5-touch cadence.

<!-- wbl-text:0118 -->
Use the example structure above. Customize hook based on your target's pain (you choose imaginary pain).

<!-- wbl-text:0119 -->
Build the qualification rubric.

<!-- wbl-text:0120 -->
When someone replies, what 3 questions do you ask to qualify? Use BANT (Budget, Authority, Need, Timeline) or simpler: "What's the pain? Who decides? When?"

<!-- wbl-text:0121 -->
Practice 3 reply scenarios.

<!-- wbl-text:0122 -->
(1) "Yes interested" → next step. (2) "Maybe later" → nurture path. (3) "Not interested" → respectful close. Write a canned reply for each.

Universal rule

<!-- wbl-text:0209 -->
For every archetype:

<!-- wbl-text:0210 -->
cold outreach is research first, message second.

<!-- wbl-text:0211 -->
The 2 minutes you spend on prospecting research is why the message lands. If the research is thin ("dear sir/madam"), the reply rate is also thin (zero). If the research is specific ("saw your post on X last week"), 5-8% reply rate becomes possible immediately.
