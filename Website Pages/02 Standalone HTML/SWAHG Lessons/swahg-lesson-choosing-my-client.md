Choosing My Client

<!-- wbl-text:0015 -->
"You do not just take a job offer." Measure first whether you and the client are a good fit.

<!-- wbl-text:0016 -->
Length:

<!-- wbl-text:0017 -->
18 minutes

<!-- wbl-text:0018 -->
For:

<!-- wbl-text:0019 -->
Anyone with multiple offers, or struggling with a current client

<!-- wbl-text:0020 -->
Updated:

<!-- wbl-text:0021 -->
2026-05-15 (v2)

<!-- wbl-text:0022 -->
Replaces:

<!-- wbl-text:0023 -->
2019 video lesson

<!-- wbl-text:0024 -->
"I was onboarded by a client, but after only two weeks I am already miserable. Should I resign?"

<!-- wbl-text:0025 -->
Common question from new SWAHG learners

<!-- wbl-text:0026 -->
Friend, many people in SWAHG have gone through this same situation. The "take any offer" mindset can make sense for the very first client. But after that,

<!-- wbl-text:0027 -->
you do not just take a job offer. You check first if it fits the stage you are in.

<!-- wbl-text:0028 -->
Today we will walk through four business types so you know what to say no to.

<!-- wbl-text:0029 -->
The wrong question vs the right question

<!-- wbl-text:0030 -->
Wrong question:

<!-- wbl-text:0031 -->
"How much is the pay?"

<!-- wbl-text:0032 -->
Better question:

<!-- wbl-text:0033 -->
"What kind of business is this, what growth stage are they in, what is their communication culture, and can I realistically deliver what they need this quarter?"

<!-- wbl-text:0034 -->
The 4 business types you'll encounter

<!-- wbl-text:0035 -->
Type

<!-- wbl-text:0036 -->
What they need

<!-- wbl-text:0037 -->
Best fit for

<!-- wbl-text:0038 -->
1. Solopreneur / Startup founder

<!-- wbl-text:0039 -->
Generalist VA who can wear many hats. Fast pace. Direct comms with founder.

<!-- wbl-text:0040 -->
Generalist Admin · Solo Entrepreneur · Polished (jr-tier)

<!-- wbl-text:0041 -->
2. Small Business (5-50 emps)

<!-- wbl-text:0042 -->
Specialist in 1-2 areas. SOPs exist. Reports to manager, not owner.

<!-- wbl-text:0043 -->
Transitioner · Polished · Creative

<!-- wbl-text:0044 -->
3. Mid-Market Company (50-500)

<!-- wbl-text:0045 -->
Highly specialized roles. Tools-heavy. Bureaucratic. Long onboarding.

<!-- wbl-text:0046 -->
Polished Freelancer · Specialized Skill seekers

<!-- wbl-text:0047 -->
4. Agency / Outsourcing firm

<!-- wbl-text:0048 -->
Manage their end client. White-label work. Strict deliverables.

<!-- wbl-text:0049 -->
Creative · Transitioner · Generalist with wedge

<!-- wbl-text:0050 -->
Red flags across all 4 types

<!-- wbl-text:0051 -->
No structured onboarding. Pays weeks late. Constantly changes scope. Rude communication. Refuses to give a review timeline.

<!-- wbl-text:0052 -->
If there are two or more red flags, do not force yourself to deserve that stress. Decline.

<!-- wbl-text:0053 -->
Example: a common client-fit progression

<!-- wbl-text:0054 -->
Example client journey (typical Filipino freelancer trajectory)

<!-- wbl-text:0055 -->
First client (Year 1)

<!-- wbl-text:0056 -->
Often a solopreneur founder. Low rate. Took anything. Stayed several months. The grit lesson.

<!-- wbl-text:0057 -->
Clients 2-4 (Year 1-2)

<!-- wbl-text:0058 -->
Mix of small business + solopreneur. Starts filtering by communication culture.

<!-- wbl-text:0059 -->
A red-flag client

<!-- wbl-text:0060 -->
Eventually you fire one for constant scope creep. Saves emotional energy.

<!-- wbl-text:0061 -->
Mature fit (later years)

<!-- wbl-text:0062 -->
Mid-market, structured, stable. Often takes years of iteration to earn.

<!-- wbl-text:0063 -->
The honest moment

<!-- wbl-text:0064 -->
Saying "I'm not the right fit" to a paying client feels like betrayal the first time. It rarely is. The "good clients are scarce" mindset is the trap.

<!-- wbl-text:0065 -->
There are more good clients than fear tells you. Clarity about fit opens better matches.

<!-- wbl-text:0066 -->
The warning the old video did not teach

<!-- wbl-text:0067 -->
The "dream client" syndrome

<!-- wbl-text:0068 -->
A Fresh Starter may need to take any reasonable first offer. A Polished Freelancer can go too far in the opposite direction and reject anything that is not a dream client.

<!-- wbl-text:0069 -->
The balance is standards yes, perfectionism no.

<!-- wbl-text:0070 -->
If no client fits your filter for three months, lower the bar by 20%.

<!-- wbl-text:0071 -->
Practice. Build your client filter. 18 minutes.

<!-- wbl-text:0072 -->
Open

<!-- wbl-text:0073 -->
Google Sheets

<!-- wbl-text:0074 -->
.

<!-- wbl-text:0075 -->
Title: "My Client Filter."

<!-- wbl-text:0076 -->
List 5 client must-haves.

<!-- wbl-text:0077 -->
(Examples: structured onboarding, weekly check-in, paid within 7 days, scope written, async OK.)

<!-- wbl-text:0078 -->
List 5 client must-avoid.

<!-- wbl-text:0079 -->
(Examples: ghosting, weekend emergencies, scope creep, hourly negotiation drama, 1-star LinkedIn reviews.)

<!-- wbl-text:0080 -->
Audit current client (or last) against the list.

<!-- wbl-text:0081 -->
Score 0-10. Below 5 = exit plan.

<!-- wbl-text:0082 -->
Audit each new offer using the filter.

<!-- wbl-text:0083 -->
Below 6 = decline. Above 8 = priority.

<!-- wbl-text:0084 -->
Action items, based on your archetype

<!-- wbl-text:0085 -->
🌟 The Polished Freelancer

<!-- wbl-text:0086 -->
~25%

<!-- wbl-text:0087 -->
Past the "take anything" stage. Build deliberate roster.

<!-- wbl-text:0088 -->
Do this week

<!-- wbl-text:0089 -->
Audit current 3-5 clients.

<!-- wbl-text:0090 -->
Score each. Drop the lowest 1 in Q3.

<!-- wbl-text:0091 -->
Define ideal client profile (ICP).

<!-- wbl-text:0092 -->
Industry + size + comms style + budget tier.

<!-- wbl-text:0093 -->
Apply only to roles matching ICP.

<!-- wbl-text:0094 -->
Quality over volume.

<!-- wbl-text:0095 -->
💼 The Corporate Transitioner

<!-- wbl-text:0096 -->
~30%

<!-- wbl-text:0097 -->
Default to small business / mid-market with structured operations.

<!-- wbl-text:0098 -->
Do this week

<!-- wbl-text:0099 -->
Target US-based small business with night-shift overlap.

<!-- wbl-text:0100 -->
Familiar BPO rhythm.

<!-- wbl-text:0101 -->
Filter out solopreneur with no SOPs.

<!-- wbl-text:0102 -->
Chaos doesn't fit your discipline.

<!-- wbl-text:0103 -->
Ask about onboarding structure in interview.

<!-- wbl-text:0104 -->
No structure = pass.

<!-- wbl-text:0105 -->
🎨 The Creative Specialist

<!-- wbl-text:0106 -->
~15%

<!-- wbl-text:0107 -->
Small business + agency are best. Solopreneur risky if no taste.

<!-- wbl-text:0108 -->
Do this week

<!-- wbl-text:0109 -->
Ask for sample of client's existing brand work.

<!-- wbl-text:0110 -->
If the client has no taste or direction, you may end up doing heavy work with endless revisions.

<!-- wbl-text:0111 -->
Require revision cap in contract.

<!-- wbl-text:0112 -->
Maximum 3 rounds. Not unlimited.

<!-- wbl-text:0113 -->
Prefer agency clients.

<!-- wbl-text:0114 -->
They know how to brief creative work.

<!-- wbl-text:0115 -->
🛒 The Solo Entrepreneur

<!-- wbl-text:0116 -->
~15%

<!-- wbl-text:0117 -->
Best fit with e-commerce small business + agency.

<!-- wbl-text:0118 -->
Do this week

<!-- wbl-text:0119 -->
Target Shopify / Amazon FBA small businesses.

<!-- wbl-text:0120 -->
Familiar territory.

<!-- wbl-text:0121 -->
Filter out clients with no inventory system.

<!-- wbl-text:0122 -->
The "I will figure it out as we go" client is a red flag.

<!-- wbl-text:0123 -->
Position as "VA who's been on the operator side."

<!-- wbl-text:0124 -->
Higher fit, higher rate.

<!-- wbl-text:0125 -->
📋 The Generalist Admin

<!-- wbl-text:0126 -->
~10%

<!-- wbl-text:0127 -->
Best with small business that needs your wedge + adjacent skills.

<!-- wbl-text:0128 -->
Do this week

<!-- wbl-text:0129 -->
Match wedge to business type.

<!-- wbl-text:0130 -->
Bookkeeping → SMB w/ revenue. Calendar EA → exec at mid-market.

<!-- wbl-text:0131 -->
Avoid jobs labeled "I need help with everything."

<!-- wbl-text:0132 -->
That is scope creep waiting to happen.

<!-- wbl-text:0133 -->
Negotiate retainer hours upfront.

<!-- wbl-text:0134 -->
Do not leave it at "see how it goes."

<!-- wbl-text:0135 -->
🌱 The Fresh Starter

<!-- wbl-text:0136 -->
~5%

<!-- wbl-text:0137 -->
First client = take whatever's reasonable. But screen out abusive.

<!-- wbl-text:0138 -->
Do this week

<!-- wbl-text:0139 -->
For first client, focus on learning + getting paid.

<!-- wbl-text:0140 -->
Do not optimize too early.

<!-- wbl-text:0141 -->
3 minimum standards:

<!-- wbl-text:0142 -->
pays on time, clear scope, respectful comms.

<!-- wbl-text:0143 -->
Below 3 = walk away.

<!-- wbl-text:0144 -->
Even if it's your first offer.
