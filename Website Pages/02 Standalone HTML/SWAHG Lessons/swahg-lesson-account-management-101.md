Account Management 101

<!-- wbl-text:0015 -->
The skill that converts "I got hired" into "they keep renewing me every quarter." Cadence + proposal anatomy + scope discipline + retention.

<!-- wbl-text:0016 -->
Length:

<!-- wbl-text:0017 -->
30 minutes

<!-- wbl-text:0018 -->
For:

<!-- wbl-text:0019 -->
VAs with at least 1 active retainer; aiming Account Manager roles

<!-- wbl-text:0020 -->
Updated:

<!-- wbl-text:0021 -->
2026-05-15 (v1)

<!-- wbl-text:0022 -->
Prerequisite:

<!-- wbl-text:0023 -->
Onboarding · Choosing My Client

<!-- wbl-text:0024 -->
"My client keeps asking for more work, but the pay is not increasing. How do I say it?"

<!-- wbl-text:0025 -->
Common question from new SWAHG learners

<!-- wbl-text:0026 -->
This is the most common scenario in the second to fourth month of a new VA retainer. The skill of

<!-- wbl-text:0027 -->
handling scope creep without losing the client

<!-- wbl-text:0028 -->
is what separates "got hired" from "kept the client." Account Management is not "salesperson." It is

<!-- wbl-text:0029 -->
steward of the relationship

<!-- wbl-text:0030 -->
. This lesson covers the 5 disciplines that protect both you and the client.

<!-- wbl-text:0031 -->
The wrong question vs the right question

<!-- wbl-text:0032 -->
Wrong question:

<!-- wbl-text:0033 -->
"How do I upsell my client?"

<!-- wbl-text:0034 -->
Better question:

<!-- wbl-text:0035 -->
"What cadence, contracts, and conversations will make the relationship predictable for both sides?"

<!-- wbl-text:0036 -->
Account management is not selling.

<!-- wbl-text:0037 -->
It is reducing surprise.

<!-- wbl-text:0038 -->
Weekly updates kill surprise. Clear scope kills surprise. Quarterly review kills surprise. The magic of senior VAs is not charm. The magic is predictability.

<!-- wbl-text:0039 -->
The 5 disciplines of Account Management

<!-- wbl-text:0040 -->
Discipline

<!-- wbl-text:0041 -->
What it does

<!-- wbl-text:0042 -->
Cadence

<!-- wbl-text:0043 -->
1. Weekly check-in

<!-- wbl-text:0044 -->
Standing call or async update. 3 sections: completed (last week), in progress (this week), questions/decisions needed (max 3). Predictable rhythm.

<!-- wbl-text:0045 -->
Every Friday or Monday

<!-- wbl-text:0046 -->
2. Documented scope

<!-- wbl-text:0047 -->
1-page scope doc updated quarterly. Lists: deliverables, frequency, exclusions. Single source of truth. You no longer have to wonder "is this included?" because it is written.

<!-- wbl-text:0048 -->
Quarterly review

<!-- wbl-text:0049 -->
3. Scope creep response

<!-- wbl-text:0050 -->
When client requests beyond scope: acknowledge → quote → confirm. "Yes, I can do that. It would be X additional hours / Y additional fee. Should I proceed?" Honest, not defensive.

<!-- wbl-text:0051 -->
As it happens

<!-- wbl-text:0052 -->
4. Monthly retainer report

<!-- wbl-text:0053 -->
Visual summary: hours billed, top deliverables, key wins. 1-page PDF or Loom video. The report justifies the invoice before the client has to ask.

<!-- wbl-text:0054 -->
End of each month

<!-- wbl-text:0055 -->
5. Quarterly business review

<!-- wbl-text:0056 -->
30-60 min strategic conversation. Review last 3 months, plan next 3. Discuss expansion or pause. This session is where retention compounds.

<!-- wbl-text:0057 -->
Every 3 months

<!-- wbl-text:0058 -->
The retention math

<!-- wbl-text:0059 -->
Average VA churn rate (industry benchmark):

<!-- wbl-text:0060 -->
~40% in first 90 days, ~25% per year after

<!-- wbl-text:0061 -->
. Implementing the 5 disciplines can drop first-90-day churn to ~10-15%. That is the equivalent of extending lifetime value 3-4x without finding more clients.

<!-- wbl-text:0062 -->
Example: a common solid modern proposal anatomy

<!-- wbl-text:0063 -->
1-page proposal structure for a $1,500/month retainer (2026 standard)

<!-- wbl-text:0064 -->
Section 1 · Objective (2-3 sentences)

<!-- wbl-text:0065 -->
Name the client's outcome, not your tasks. "Help [Client] grow Instagram engagement from 1.5% to 3% over 90 days while reclaiming 15 hours/week of founder time."

<!-- wbl-text:0066 -->
Section 2 · Deliverables (5-8 bullets)

<!-- wbl-text:0067 -->
Specific outputs: "20 IG posts/month · 8 Reels edits · 12 newsletter drafts · weekly content calendar · monthly analytics report." Quantified, not vague.

<!-- wbl-text:0068 -->
Section 3 · Process (3-4 bullets)

<!-- wbl-text:0069 -->
How work happens: "Weekly content batch every Monday · Friday async check-in · monthly Zoom review · Trello shared board for visibility."

<!-- wbl-text:0070 -->
Section 4 · Investment

<!-- wbl-text:0071 -->
"$1,500/month, billed first of each month, net-7 payment terms. 90-day initial commitment, month-to-month after."

<!-- wbl-text:0072 -->
Section 5 · Scope boundaries

<!-- wbl-text:0073 -->
What's NOT included: ad spend management, paid sponsorships, live-event coverage. Optional add-ons listed separately with their own rates.

<!-- wbl-text:0074 -->
Section 6 · Next steps

<!-- wbl-text:0075 -->
"Reply 'approved' to start. Onboarding kit will be sent within 1 business day. First deliverable in Week 2."

<!-- wbl-text:0076 -->
The architecture insight

<!-- wbl-text:0077 -->
A proposal is

<!-- wbl-text:0078 -->
a one-page contract translation

<!-- wbl-text:0079 -->
. The old "20-page proposal with case studies" is obsolete in 2026. Modern proposals: 1 page, scannable, focused on the client's outcome. You are not trying to over-convince. The outcome should be convincing enough.

<!-- wbl-text:0080 -->
The warnings people usually skip

<!-- wbl-text:0081 -->
Silent scope creep is the killer

<!-- wbl-text:0082 -->
"Can you just add 1 extra task here?" eventually becomes 5 extra hours/week. By month 3, you are billing the same rate for 50% more work. Resentment builds. The client wonders why you are slower. Both are bad outcomes.

<!-- wbl-text:0083 -->
Surface every scope creep immediately

<!-- wbl-text:0084 -->
with the acknowledge-quote-confirm framework. Not after 3 weeks.

<!-- wbl-text:0085 -->
Do not "discount" your way to retention

<!-- wbl-text:0086 -->
When a client signals "tight budget," the tendency is to lower rate to keep them. Trap. Discounting trains them: "you can ask for less and she'll say yes." If there is a budget issue, it is better to

<!-- wbl-text:0087 -->
reduce scope

<!-- wbl-text:0088 -->
at the same hourly rate than keep the same scope at a lower hourly rate. Protect the dignity of your rate.

<!-- wbl-text:0089 -->
Practice. 30 minutes, build your AM toolkit.

<!-- wbl-text:0090 -->
Draft a weekly check-in template.

<!-- wbl-text:0091 -->
3 sections: Completed (5 bullets), In Progress (3 bullets), Questions/Decisions (max 3). Save in Google Doc.

<!-- wbl-text:0092 -->
Draft a 1-page scope doc template.

<!-- wbl-text:0093 -->
Deliverables, frequency, exclusions. Reusable across clients with name+detail swap.

<!-- wbl-text:0094 -->
Practice the scope creep response.

<!-- wbl-text:0095 -->
Imagine client says "can you also do X?" Write 3 candidate replies. Pick the best one. Save as a Slack/email canned response.

<!-- wbl-text:0096 -->
Build a monthly retainer report template.

<!-- wbl-text:0097 -->
1-page Google Doc. Sections: hours worked, top 3 wins, deliverables completed, suggested next 30 days. Reusable.

<!-- wbl-text:0098 -->
Draft a quarterly business review agenda.

<!-- wbl-text:0099 -->
5 questions for client + 5 metrics to show + 1 forward-looking proposal. Aim 30-60 min total.

<!-- wbl-text:0100 -->
Build a 1-page proposal template

<!-- wbl-text:0101 -->
using the 6 sections above. Save as Google Doc + PDF version.

Action items, based on your archetype

<!-- wbl-text:0110 -->
🌟 The Polished Freelancer

<!-- wbl-text:0111 -->
~25% · senior account manager

<!-- wbl-text:0112 -->
Multiple clients = multiple accounts to manage. The difference between $20/hr and $40/hr is account management discipline.

<!-- wbl-text:0113 -->
Do this week

<!-- wbl-text:0114 -->
Audit each active client's "discipline coverage."

<!-- wbl-text:0115 -->
Weekly check-in (yes/no), documented scope (yes/no), monthly report (yes/no), quarterly review (yes/no). Fill gaps starting next week.

<!-- wbl-text:0116 -->
Build a "client P

<!-- wbl-text:0117 -->
" view in your tracking.

<!-- wbl-text:0118 -->
Hours billed, hours actually worked, effective hourly rate per client. The data tells you who to expand and who to release.

<!-- wbl-text:0119 -->
Conduct 1 quarterly review this month

<!-- wbl-text:0120 -->
with your longest client. That session is where rate raises happen.

<!-- wbl-text:0121 -->
Recommended target:

<!-- wbl-text:0122 -->
Senior Account Manager / Fractional COO · ~$30-50/hr · 2-3 retainers max, deep relationships.

<!-- wbl-text:0123 -->
💼 The Corporate Transitioner

<!-- wbl-text:0124 -->
~30% · client services natural fit

<!-- wbl-text:0125 -->
Your BPO client services background is a perfect match. Difference: smaller client list, deeper relationship.

<!-- wbl-text:0126 -->
Do this week

<!-- wbl-text:0127 -->
Translate "client service" muscle to freelance.

<!-- wbl-text:0128 -->
Same SLA discipline, NPS-style satisfaction check, escalation flow. Apply at smaller scale.

<!-- wbl-text:0129 -->
Build a "client health" tracker.

<!-- wbl-text:0130 -->
Per client: response time, hours used vs scoped, satisfaction signal (proxy: how often client thanks vs corrects). Weekly review.

<!-- wbl-text:0131 -->
Practice the "calm during scope creep" response.

<!-- wbl-text:0132 -->
BPO trained you in this. Bring the muscle to freelance scope creep.

<!-- wbl-text:0133 -->
Recommended target:

<!-- wbl-text:0134 -->
Client Services VA · ~$15-25/hr · 3-5 retainers, medium-touch.

<!-- wbl-text:0135 -->
🎨 The Creative Specialist

<!-- wbl-text:0136 -->
~15% · creative AM is rare premium

<!-- wbl-text:0137 -->
Creative VAs who also handle account management earn $40-60/hr. The combination is rare and valuable.

<!-- wbl-text:0138 -->
Do this week

<!-- wbl-text:0139 -->
Build a "creative deliverables tracker."

<!-- wbl-text:0140 -->
Per client: campaigns delivered, engagement metrics, asset reuse rate. Data sells expansion.

<!-- wbl-text:0141 -->
Practice presenting work visually.

<!-- wbl-text:0142 -->
Loom walkthroughs of campaigns, before-after engagement screenshots, mood-board pivots. Visual presentation sells better.

<!-- wbl-text:0143 -->
Position as "Creative Director-Lite."

<!-- wbl-text:0144 -->
This framing is senior + creative + business-aware. Pay band reflects it.

<!-- wbl-text:0145 -->
Recommended target:

<!-- wbl-text:0146 -->
Fractional Creative Director · ~$30-50/hr · 1-2 deep retainers.

<!-- wbl-text:0147 -->
🛒 The Solo Entrepreneur

<!-- wbl-text:0148 -->
~15% · business owner empathy

<!-- wbl-text:0149 -->
Your shop owner background is your secret weapon. You speak business owner, not freelancer.

<!-- wbl-text:0150 -->
Do this week

<!-- wbl-text:0151 -->
Frame check-ins around "business outcome," not "tasks done."

<!-- wbl-text:0152 -->
A shop owner client cares about revenue + customer retention + inventory turnover. Map your work to those.

<!-- wbl-text:0153 -->
Bring an "owner-to-owner" tone

<!-- wbl-text:0154 -->
sa proposals. Less corporate, more peer-to-peer.

<!-- wbl-text:0155 -->
Specialize in ecom or local business retainers.

<!-- wbl-text:0156 -->
That niche is more naturally drawn to your background.

<!-- wbl-text:0157 -->
Recommended target:

<!-- wbl-text:0158 -->
Ecom Account Manager · ~$20-35/hr · 2-3 shop retainers.

<!-- wbl-text:0159 -->
📋 The Generalist Admin

<!-- wbl-text:0160 -->
~10% · ops + AM hybrid

<!-- wbl-text:0161 -->
Admin discipline + AM skills = "Operations + Account Management" hybrid role. Premium VA tier.

<!-- wbl-text:0162 -->
Do this week

<!-- wbl-text:0163 -->
Build a master client tracker.

<!-- wbl-text:0164 -->
All clients in one Sheet: scope, cadence, retainer fee, expansion potential, renewal date. The tracker is your dashboard.

<!-- wbl-text:0165 -->
Build automated weekly report generation.

<!-- wbl-text:0166 -->
Trello/Asana → Sheet → Doc → PDF. Automation saves 30 min/week per client.

<!-- wbl-text:0167 -->
Practice the "calm boundary" voice.

<!-- wbl-text:0168 -->
Scope creep responses are harder for admin-natural-helpers to say no to. Practice 5 candidate responses until they feel natural.

<!-- wbl-text:0169 -->
Recommended target:

<!-- wbl-text:0170 -->
Operations + AM Hybrid VA · ~$20-30/hr · 3-4 retainers with automated infrastructure.

<!-- wbl-text:0171 -->
🌱 The Fresh Starter

<!-- wbl-text:0172 -->
~5% · build from first client

<!-- wbl-text:0173 -->
No client yet. AM skills are best learned WHILE managing your first retainer. Practice the templates now, apply when ready.

<!-- wbl-text:0174 -->
Do this week

<!-- wbl-text:0175 -->
Draft all 6 templates from the practice section.

<!-- wbl-text:0176 -->
Template work is portable, save it until ready.

<!-- wbl-text:0177 -->
Apply the weekly check-in cadence to SWAHG learning.

<!-- wbl-text:0178 -->
Every Friday, post "what I learned this week + what I'm working on next" in the SWAHG group. Build the habit.

<!-- wbl-text:0179 -->
Find an accountability partner.

<!-- wbl-text:0180 -->
Pair with another SWAHG learner. Practice scope conversations using imagined scenarios. Build the muscle before the actual client.

<!-- wbl-text:0181 -->
Recommended target:

<!-- wbl-text:0182 -->
Defer AM specialty until month 4-6 of first retainer. Foundation + onboarding skills first.

<!-- wbl-text:0183 -->
Universal rule

<!-- wbl-text:0184 -->
For every archetype:

<!-- wbl-text:0185 -->
relationship over rate.

<!-- wbl-text:0186 -->
The most important part of Account Management is not sales language. It is mutual respect. Without respect, there is no rate raise. With respect, every negotiation is easier. Respect is built through weekly cadence + clear scope + honest scope creep response.
