Common question from new SWAHG learners

<!-- wbl-text:0028 -->
I was like that before. I wanted the cheapest option because I was afraid of wasting money. But I learned this:

<!-- wbl-text:0029 -->
a bad computer may hurt your career growth in freelancing.

<!-- wbl-text:0030 -->
The problem is not that a good computer is expensive. The problem is that a slow computer makes you wait while your client is also waiting. And the client can choose not to keep paying you next month.

<!-- wbl-text:0031 -->
The wrong question vs the right question

<!-- wbl-text:0032 -->
Wrong question:

<!-- wbl-text:0033 -->
"What is the cheapest laptop I can buy?"

<!-- wbl-text:0034 -->
Better question:

<!-- wbl-text:0035 -->
"What computer can keep up with my first three clients, last for three years, and still respect my family budget today?"

<!-- wbl-text:0036 -->
The first question gives you a laptop. The second gives you a

<!-- wbl-text:0037 -->
career foundation

<!-- wbl-text:0038 -->
. Every box in the

<!-- wbl-text:0039 -->
Blueprint

<!-- wbl-text:0040 -->
depends on this.

The 6 criteria of a good freelancer computer

<!-- wbl-text:0042 -->
This is the checklist I use when someone asks for advice in the SWAHG group. I do not start with specs. I ask what

<!-- wbl-text:0043 -->
job

<!-- wbl-text:0044 -->
your machine needs to handle.

<!-- wbl-text:0045 -->
What

<!-- wbl-text:0046 -->
Why it matters

<!-- wbl-text:0047 -->
Minimum to ship

<!-- wbl-text:0048 -->
RAM (memory)

<!-- wbl-text:0049 -->
If memory is too low, the laptop can freeze when you are on Zoom while Chrome and

<!-- wbl-text:0050 -->
Canva

<!-- wbl-text:0051 -->
are open

<!-- wbl-text:0052 -->
8 GB minimum, 16 GB target

<!-- wbl-text:0053 -->
Storage (SSD)

<!-- wbl-text:0054 -->
An old HDD will feel painfully slow. SSD is the standard now.

<!-- wbl-text:0055 -->
256 GB SSD floor, 512 GB target

<!-- wbl-text:0056 -->
Processor

<!-- wbl-text:0057 -->
A decent chip so client video calls do not leave you behind

<!-- wbl-text:0058 -->
Intel i5 / AMD Ryzen 5 / Apple M-series

<!-- wbl-text:0059 -->
Battery life

<!-- wbl-text:0060 -->
During a brownout, you should still have time to message the client

<!-- wbl-text:0061 -->
6+ hours real use

<!-- wbl-text:0062 -->
Webcam + mic

<!-- wbl-text:0063 -->
For interviews, "audio only" should not be your excuse

<!-- wbl-text:0064 -->
720p+ webcam, working mic

<!-- wbl-text:0065 -->
Keyboard + screen

<!-- wbl-text:0066 -->
You will use it for eight hours. If the setup is poor, your back and eyes will suffer.

<!-- wbl-text:0067 -->
13"+ screen, full keyboard

<!-- wbl-text:0068 -->
Practical truth

<!-- wbl-text:0069 -->
You don't need the most expensive one. You need one that

<!-- wbl-text:0070 -->
can run for a long period of time

<!-- wbl-text:0071 -->
doing what your future client will ask. The cheapest laptop on

<!-- wbl-text:0072 -->
Lazada

<!-- wbl-text:0073 -->
usually fails on RAM + SSD. A refurbished 2-year-old machine with 16 GB RAM almost always beats a brand-new ₱20K laptop with 4 GB.

<!-- wbl-text:0074 -->
Example: an honest freelancer setup

<!-- wbl-text:0075 -->
Not every "perfect setup" picture online is real. Most working freelancers have limitations too. Let's do honest math on what a solid setup looks like.

<!-- wbl-text:0076 -->
Example daily-driver pattern

<!-- wbl-text:0077 -->
A common solid pick: a mid-tier

<!-- wbl-text:0078 -->
MacBook Pro

<!-- wbl-text:0079 -->
or Windows equivalent with Apple M-series / Intel i5+ / AMD Ryzen 5+

<!-- wbl-text:0080 -->
Memory

<!-- wbl-text:0081 -->
16 GB minimum for serious work · 32 GB recommended if budget allows

<!-- wbl-text:0082 -->
Storage

<!-- wbl-text:0083 -->
512 GB SSD target · 1 TB if you store client video / design files

<!-- wbl-text:0084 -->
OS

<!-- wbl-text:0085 -->
Keep up to date for security + client-app compatibility

<!-- wbl-text:0086 -->
Watch for memory hogs

<!-- wbl-text:0087 -->
Chrome

<!-- wbl-text:0088 -->
with 30+ tabs ·

<!-- wbl-text:0089 -->
Notion

<!-- wbl-text:0090 -->
· video editors · multiple chat apps

<!-- wbl-text:0091 -->
A common pattern: pair a portable laptop with an older but upgradeable desktop. Older Intel

<!-- wbl-text:0092 -->
iMacs

<!-- wbl-text:0093 -->
and many Windows desktops let you swap RAM via

<!-- wbl-text:0094 -->
OWC SO-DIMM

<!-- wbl-text:0095 -->
-style upgrades. Older hardware with upgradeable RAM often outlasts a brand-new cheap laptop with soldered 4 GB.

<!-- wbl-text:0096 -->
The honest moment

<!-- wbl-text:0097 -->
16 GB RAM feels like plenty until you're running a code editor + Chrome + 30 browser tabs + a chat app. Then you hit the ceiling, the machine swaps, everything slows.

<!-- wbl-text:0098 -->
If you can reach 32 GB, choose it. Especially for Apple Silicon, because you cannot upgrade it later.

<!-- wbl-text:0099 -->
The warning I sometimes forgot to say

<!-- wbl-text:0100 -->
This is not drama

<!-- wbl-text:0101 -->
If you buy a laptop with too little RAM just because it is cheap, this is what can happen: you accept an interview. You join Zoom. The computer freezes. The audio cuts out. Later that night, the client tells you, "let's go with someone else, their setup is more stable." Your skill did not fail. Your tools did.

<!-- wbl-text:0102 -->
That's the consequence we don't talk about enough.

<!-- wbl-text:0103 -->
I am not saying money is the most important thing. I am saying that if you save ₱5,000 today but lose a ₱30,000/month client because of it, that is not savings. That is

<!-- wbl-text:0104 -->
expensive economy.

<!-- wbl-text:0105 -->
Practice. Do this now, not tomorrow.

<!-- wbl-text:0106 -->
Open the computer you are using right now. Follow this 5-step audit. It only takes 12 minutes.

<!-- wbl-text:0107 -->
Check RAM.

<!-- wbl-text:0108 -->
Mac: Apple menu → About This Mac. Windows: Settings → System → About. Remember how much RAM you have.

<!-- wbl-text:0109 -->
Check storage.

<!-- wbl-text:0110 -->
Mac: About This Mac → Storage. Windows: Settings → Storage. How much space is free?

<!-- wbl-text:0111 -->
Check chip / processor.

<!-- wbl-text:0112 -->
Same About screen. May "Intel Core i3" ba? "Apple M1"? "Ryzen 5"?

<!-- wbl-text:0113 -->
Plug out the charger. Time your battery.

<!-- wbl-text:0114 -->
Do this while browsing

<!-- wbl-text:0115 -->
OnlineJobs.ph

<!-- wbl-text:0116 -->
. If the battery drops 50% in less than 2 hours, it is already weak.

<!-- wbl-text:0117 -->
Open a video call to yourself.

<!-- wbl-text:0118 -->
Open

<!-- wbl-text:0119 -->
Google Meet

<!-- wbl-text:0120 -->
. Check: does the mic work? Webcam? Are you too dark because the room light is poor?

<!-- wbl-text:0121 -->
The audit form:

<!-- wbl-text:0122 -->
Write this in a notebook, yes, on paper:

<!-- wbl-text:0123 -->
My RAM: ____ GB

<!-- wbl-text:0124 -->
My storage: ____ GB free of ____ GB total

<!-- wbl-text:0125 -->
My processor: ____

<!-- wbl-text:0126 -->
My battery: lasted ____ minutes out of unplugged

<!-- wbl-text:0127 -->
My webcam: ☐ works ☐ blurry ☐ broken

<!-- wbl-text:0128 -->
My mic: ☐ clear ☐ hissy ☐ broken

🌟 The Polished Freelancer

<!-- wbl-text:0137 -->
~25% · proceed ~80%

<!-- wbl-text:0138 -->
Already running your own client roster. Multi-platform. Articulates pricing without flinching.

<!-- wbl-text:0139 -->
Do this week

<!-- wbl-text:0140 -->
Audit your current machine against client-load math.

<!-- wbl-text:0141 -->
If you already have 3+ active clients, 16 GB RAM should be your minimum. If you are still on 8 GB, plan an upgrade within 6 months.

<!-- wbl-text:0142 -->
Add a backup machine.

<!-- wbl-text:0143 -->
Buy a refurbished ChromeBook or older iMac from

<!-- wbl-text:0144 -->
Swappa

<!-- wbl-text:0145 -->
as failover. If your primary machine breaks, you will not lose income.

<!-- wbl-text:0146 -->
Pre-claim the upgrade.

<!-- wbl-text:0147 -->
Put this in your

<!-- wbl-text:0148 -->
Google Sheets

<!-- wbl-text:0149 -->
budget: "Q3 2026, 32 GB MacBook upgrade, paid from retainer #3." Make it a business expense, not personal spending.

<!-- wbl-text:0150 -->
Recommended pick:

<!-- wbl-text:0151 -->
Refurbished

<!-- wbl-text:0152 -->
Apple Certified Refurbished

<!-- wbl-text:0153 -->
14" MacBook Pro M3 16 GB · 1 TB · ~₱90-110K. Pay with client money and record it in your books.

<!-- wbl-text:0154 -->
💼 The Corporate Transitioner

<!-- wbl-text:0155 -->
~30% · proceed ~60%

<!-- wbl-text:0156 -->
BPO English plus office discipline. Strong from years of phone work. Needs the freelancing bridge.

<!-- wbl-text:0157 -->
Do this week

<!-- wbl-text:0158 -->
Don't quit your job to buy a computer.

<!-- wbl-text:0159 -->
Finish your notice period first. Use your 13th month pay plus leave conversion to buy the machine.

<!-- wbl-text:0160 -->
Mid-range Windows or refurbished MacBook Air.

<!-- wbl-text:0161 -->
You do not need top-tier yet. A 16 GB Windows laptop from the

<!-- wbl-text:0162 -->
Lenovo

<!-- wbl-text:0163 -->
ThinkPad refurbished line, ~₱35-45K, will last 4 years.

<!-- wbl-text:0164 -->
Verify your home internet first.

<!-- wbl-text:0165 -->
Run

<!-- wbl-text:0166 -->
Speedtest

<!-- wbl-text:0167 -->
. If under 25 Mbps, get a backup mobile hotspot before you buy the laptop. The internet failure rate is 45% in the SWAHG candidate pool.

<!-- wbl-text:0168 -->
Recommended pick:

<!-- wbl-text:0169 -->
Lenovo ThinkPad T-series

<!-- wbl-text:0170 -->
refurb 16 GB · 512 GB SSD · i5, OR

<!-- wbl-text:0171 -->
MacBook Air M2

<!-- wbl-text:0172 -->
16 GB · 256 GB if budget allows.

<!-- wbl-text:0173 -->
🎨 The Creative Specialist

<!-- wbl-text:0174 -->
~15% · proceed ~70%

<!-- wbl-text:0175 -->
Photoshop, Premiere, video editing. Portfolio is your wedge. Admin tooling thin.

<!-- wbl-text:0176 -->
Do this week

<!-- wbl-text:0177 -->
Your machine must run

<!-- wbl-text:0178 -->
Adobe Creative Cloud

<!-- wbl-text:0179 -->
+

<!-- wbl-text:0180 -->
CapCut

<!-- wbl-text:0181 -->
without choking.

<!-- wbl-text:0182 -->
16 GB RAM is the floor, not the target. Choose 32 GB if you do 4K video. 8 GB is not enough, period.

<!-- wbl-text:0183 -->
External SSD non-negotiable.

<!-- wbl-text:0184 -->
Add a

<!-- wbl-text:0185 -->
SanDisk Extreme Portable SSD

<!-- wbl-text:0186 -->
1 TB (~₱5,500). Put project files there, not on internal storage.

<!-- wbl-text:0187 -->
Color-accurate display matters.

<!-- wbl-text:0188 -->

<!-- wbl-text:0189 -->
DisplayCAL

<!-- wbl-text:0190 -->
and check

<!-- wbl-text:0191 -->
macOS Display settings

<!-- wbl-text:0192 -->

<!-- wbl-text:0193 -->
Recommended pick:

<!-- wbl-text:0194 -->
MacBook Pro 14" M3

<!-- wbl-text:0195 -->
16 GB · 512 GB (₱115-130K) OR refurbished M1 Max 32 GB · 1 TB from

<!-- wbl-text:0196 -->
OWC

<!-- wbl-text:0197 -->
.

<!-- wbl-text:0198 -->
🛒 The Solo Entrepreneur

<!-- wbl-text:0199 -->
~15% · proceed ~55%

<!-- wbl-text:0200 -->
Ran your own ecom or store. Self-taught Canva + Shopify + Mailchimp. Often a mom with scheduling considerations.

<!-- wbl-text:0201 -->
Do this week

<!-- wbl-text:0202 -->
Treat it as a business expense.

<!-- wbl-text:0203 -->
Keep the receipt and include it in your income tax filing. The laptop is depreciable equipment, not personal spending.

<!-- wbl-text:0204 -->
Pick something the kids cannot break.

<!-- wbl-text:0205 -->
Do not leave a MacBook Air on the coffee table. Store it in a locker or inside a cabinet when not in use. ChromeBook is the budget-friendly, sturdy option.

<!-- wbl-text:0206 -->
Add scheduling tools to it day one.

<!-- wbl-text:0207 -->
Install

<!-- wbl-text:0208 -->
Google Calendar

<!-- wbl-text:0209 -->
,

<!-- wbl-text:0210 -->
Trello

<!-- wbl-text:0211 -->
, and

<!-- wbl-text:0212 -->
Toggl Track

<!-- wbl-text:0213 -->
on the first day. Use the business-running skill you already have for client work.

<!-- wbl-text:0214 -->
Recommended pick:

<!-- wbl-text:0215 -->
Acer ChromeBook Spin

<!-- wbl-text:0216 -->
8 GB · 128 GB (~₱20-25K) for kid-proof daily, OR

<!-- wbl-text:0217 -->
ASUS VivoBook

<!-- wbl-text:0218 -->
16 GB if budget supports.

<!-- wbl-text:0219 -->
📋 The Generalist Admin

<!-- wbl-text:0220 -->
~10% · proceed ~50%

<!-- wbl-text:0221 -->
Excel-fluent, calendar-disciplined. No specialty wedge yet.

<!-- wbl-text:0222 -->
Do this week

<!-- wbl-text:0223 -->
Match the machine to a wedge.

<!-- wbl-text:0224 -->
Bookkeeping VA needs

<!-- wbl-text:0225 -->
QuickBooks

<!-- wbl-text:0226 -->
compatibility (Windows easier). Inbox-Zero VA needs reliable webcam + mic. Pick the wedge BEFORE the laptop.

<!-- wbl-text:0227 -->
Mid-range Windows is fine.

<!-- wbl-text:0228 -->
You do not need a MacBook. A

<!-- wbl-text:0229 -->
Dell Inspiron

<!-- wbl-text:0230 -->
16 GB · 512 GB is enough for 95% of admin VA work.

<!-- wbl-text:0231 -->
Add a second monitor.

<!-- wbl-text:0232 -->
A ₱8K monitor from

<!-- wbl-text:0233 -->
Shopee

<!-- wbl-text:0234 -->
is a productivity multiplier. You can work side by side: client email on the left, your work on the right.

<!-- wbl-text:0235 -->
Recommended pick:

<!-- wbl-text:0236 -->
Dell Inspiron 15

<!-- wbl-text:0237 -->
16 GB · 512 GB i5 (~₱40-50K) OR refurbished

<!-- wbl-text:0238 -->
ThinkPad E-series

<!-- wbl-text:0239 -->
.

🌱 The Fresh Starter

<!-- wbl-text:0241 -->
~5% · proceed ~30%

<!-- wbl-text:0242 -->
Eager but unprepared. 0–2 tools. Weak English self-rating. Confidence gap is the bottleneck.

<!-- wbl-text:0243 -->
Do this week

<!-- wbl-text:0244 -->
You do not need to buy right away.

<!-- wbl-text:0245 -->
Before spending ₱30K, complete

<!-- wbl-text:0246 -->
Blueprint

<!-- wbl-text:0247 -->
stages 1-2 first. Once you are committed to the skill, that is when you buy.

<!-- wbl-text:0248 -->
Borrow first if you can.

<!-- wbl-text:0249 -->
Borrow a laptop from a sister or cousin for the first 30 days. Test whether you truly want freelance work. If you get accepted on

<!-- wbl-text:0250 -->
OnlineJobs.ph

<!-- wbl-text:0251 -->

<!-- wbl-text:0252 -->

<!-- wbl-text:0254 -->
Lazada Wallet

<!-- wbl-text:0257 -->
Refurbished

<!-- wbl-text:0258 -->
Acer Aspire

8 GB · 256 GB (~₱18-25K) for first 12 months. Upgrade to 16 GB machine after Client #2.
