Employee to Freelancer

<!-- wbl-text:0015 -->
You do not need to resign tomorrow. The jump should be a plan, not a late-night decision.

<!-- wbl-text:0016 -->
Length:

<!-- wbl-text:0017 -->
18 minutes

<!-- wbl-text:0018 -->
For:

<!-- wbl-text:0019 -->
Anyone currently employed considering full freelance

<!-- wbl-text:0020 -->
Updated:

<!-- wbl-text:0021 -->
2026-05-15 (v2)

<!-- wbl-text:0022 -->
Replaces:

<!-- wbl-text:0023 -->
2019 video lesson

<!-- wbl-text:0024 -->
"I do not want the office anymore. I already resigned. What now?"

<!-- wbl-text:0025 -->
Common question from new SWAHG learners

<!-- wbl-text:0026 -->
Friend, let us balance speed with stability. Although we love freelancing,

<!-- wbl-text:0027 -->
I do not recommend jumping straight away.

<!-- wbl-text:0028 -->
My case is different. Your situation is different. Resigning before you have a client is like jumping into a pool without knowing if there is water.

<!-- wbl-text:0029 -->
The wrong question vs the right question

<!-- wbl-text:0030 -->
Wrong question:

<!-- wbl-text:0031 -->
"When can I resign?"

<!-- wbl-text:0032 -->
Better question:

<!-- wbl-text:0033 -->
"What income level and risk profile should I be able to carry before I resign?"

<!-- wbl-text:0034 -->
The question "when" often comes from anxiety. The question "what baseline" comes from strategy. The Freelancing Journey is a marathon, not a sprint. Prepare properly so you can run longer without getting hurt.

<!-- wbl-text:0035 -->
The 5 conditions before you resign

<!-- wbl-text:0036 -->
Condition

<!-- wbl-text:0037 -->
Why it matters

<!-- wbl-text:0038 -->
Target

<!-- wbl-text:0039 -->
1. First freelance income

<!-- wbl-text:0040 -->
Proof of concept. Do not resign because "I think I can do it." Show the first ₱5,000 in the bank.

<!-- wbl-text:0041 -->
First paying client landed

<!-- wbl-text:0042 -->
2. Emergency fund

<!-- wbl-text:0043 -->
Keep 3 to 6 months of expenses in savings. When a dry spell comes, you will not panic.

<!-- wbl-text:0044 -->
3-6× monthly expenses

<!-- wbl-text:0045 -->
3. Retainer client minimum

<!-- wbl-text:0046 -->
Not project-only income. Aim for stable monthly income, such as a ₱20K to ₱30K minimum monthly retainer.

<!-- wbl-text:0047 -->
1 retainer @ ₱20K+/month

<!-- wbl-text:0048 -->
4. Health insurance plan

<!-- wbl-text:0049 -->
Employer-provided HMO will disappear. Buy personal HMO coverage or set aside emergency funds.

<!-- wbl-text:0050 -->
Personal HMO arranged

<!-- wbl-text:0051 -->
5. Family alignment

<!-- wbl-text:0052 -->
You, your spouse, or your parents should be aligned. When panic comes, you need a support system.

<!-- wbl-text:0053 -->
Family conversation completed

<!-- wbl-text:0054 -->
The bridge phase

<!-- wbl-text:0055 -->
For 3 to 6 months, your office job and first freelance client may sit side by side. Yes, it is hard. Yes, it is tiring. But that bridge phase is often the most secure transition. You avoid first-month freelance panic because stable income is still present.

<!-- wbl-text:0056 -->
Example: a healthy resignation timeline

<!-- wbl-text:0057 -->
Example transition pattern (12-18 months is common)

<!-- wbl-text:0058 -->
Month 1-3 (side hustle)

<!-- wbl-text:0059 -->
2 hrs/night on

<!-- wbl-text:0060 -->
Upwork

<!-- wbl-text:0061 -->
or similar. No client yet. Building portfolio.

<!-- wbl-text:0062 -->
Month 4-6 (first clients)

<!-- wbl-text:0063 -->
First small clients land. Income still well below office salary. Not yet enough to quit.

<!-- wbl-text:0064 -->
Month 7-12 (income matching)

<!-- wbl-text:0065 -->
Freelance income approaches 60-80% of office salary. Still working both. Sleep deprived.

<!-- wbl-text:0066 -->
Resignation point

<!-- wbl-text:0067 -->
Resign when you have ~3 retainer clients + several months emergency fund.

<!-- wbl-text:0068 -->
The honest moment

<!-- wbl-text:0069 -->
Some people resign after about 12 months of dual life. Others need 18 to 24 months. There is no perfect timeline.

<!-- wbl-text:0070 -->
The right timeline is the one you can carry without putting your family at risk.

<!-- wbl-text:0071 -->
The warning SWAHG sees in the group every quarter

<!-- wbl-text:0072 -->
The "resigned, now what?" panic

<!-- wbl-text:0073 -->
Every three months, a new SWAHG member posts something like, "I resigned today, what next?" That is the worst position: no client, no savings, and bills still coming. The pressure can push you to drop rates, sign exploitative contracts, or rush a weak portfolio.

<!-- wbl-text:0074 -->
Resign with a plan, not with a feeling.

<!-- wbl-text:0075 -->
Practice. Build your resignation calculator. 20 minutes.

<!-- wbl-text:0076 -->
Open a

<!-- wbl-text:0077 -->
Google Sheets

<!-- wbl-text:0078 -->
.

<!-- wbl-text:0079 -->
Title: "My Resignation Math."

<!-- wbl-text:0080 -->
Calculate monthly expenses:

<!-- wbl-text:0081 -->
rent + utilities + food + transport + family support + savings target. Sum.

<!-- wbl-text:0082 -->
Calculate 6-month emergency fund target.

<!-- wbl-text:0083 -->
Monthly expenses × 6 = your safety floor.

<!-- wbl-text:0084 -->
Calculate current savings.

<!-- wbl-text:0085 -->
Bank balance + emergency stash. Gap = how much more to save.

<!-- wbl-text:0086 -->
Calculate freelance-income breakeven.

<!-- wbl-text:0087 -->
Monthly expenses + tax allowance (30% buffer) = minimum freelance income before resignation.

<!-- wbl-text:0088 -->
Set your "Resign Decision Day."

<!-- wbl-text:0089 -->
When freelance income ≥ breakeven for 3 consecutive months AND emergency fund met = green light. Mark calendar.

Action items, based on your archetype

<!-- wbl-text:0091 -->
🌟 The Polished Freelancer

<!-- wbl-text:0092 -->
~25% · proceed ~80%

<!-- wbl-text:0093 -->
Probably already resigned. This lesson is about scaling protection.

<!-- wbl-text:0094 -->
Do this week

<!-- wbl-text:0095 -->
Setup 12-month emergency fund.

<!-- wbl-text:0096 -->
Freelancers need bigger buffer than employees. Aim for 12× monthly expenses long-term.

<!-- wbl-text:0097 -->
Get HMO + life insurance.

<!-- wbl-text:0098 -->
Cobra / employer-provided HMO is gone. Personal coverage critical. Check

<!-- wbl-text:0099 -->
Maxicare

<!-- wbl-text:0100 -->
or

<!-- wbl-text:0101 -->
Medicard

<!-- wbl-text:0102 -->
individual plans.

<!-- wbl-text:0103 -->
Diversify client base.

<!-- wbl-text:0104 -->
Do not let 80% of income come from one client. Aim for no client to exceed 30% of revenue.

<!-- wbl-text:0105 -->
💼 The Corporate Transitioner

<!-- wbl-text:0106 -->
~30% · proceed ~60%

<!-- wbl-text:0107 -->
Highest stakes. Job stability versus freelance dream.

<!-- wbl-text:0108 -->
Do this week

<!-- wbl-text:0109 -->
DO NOT resign yet.

<!-- wbl-text:0110 -->
Side-hustle for minimum 6 months while employed. The "I want to quit" feeling is not enough.

<!-- wbl-text:0111 -->
Document your work for portfolio.

<!-- wbl-text:0112 -->
Before resignation, screenshot everything (anonymized). Find a way to mention those achievements in your portfolio.

<!-- wbl-text:0113 -->
Inform 1 trusted friend at work.

<!-- wbl-text:0114 -->
Tell a trusted friend, not HR. Emotional support during the transition is valuable.

<!-- wbl-text:0115 -->
🎨 The Creative Specialist

<!-- wbl-text:0116 -->
~15% · proceed ~70%

<!-- wbl-text:0117 -->
Portfolio is the safety net. Build it strong before jumping.

<!-- wbl-text:0118 -->
Do this week

<!-- wbl-text:0119 -->
10-piece portfolio before resignation.

<!-- wbl-text:0120 -->
Mock projects okay, but published / shareable.

<!-- wbl-text:0121 -->
Secure 1 retainer creative client first.

<!-- wbl-text:0122 -->
Do not rely on project-only freelance income. Build a monthly base.

<!-- wbl-text:0123 -->
Setup tax tracking from Day 1.

<!-- wbl-text:0124 -->
Use

<!-- wbl-text:0125 -->
Taxumo

<!-- wbl-text:0126 -->
for PH freelancer tax filing. When you have regular income, register with the BIR.

<!-- wbl-text:0127 -->
🛒 The Solo Entrepreneur

<!-- wbl-text:0128 -->
~15% · proceed ~55%

<!-- wbl-text:0129 -->
Already self-employed. "Resignation" means closing/scaling shop.

<!-- wbl-text:0130 -->
Do this week

<!-- wbl-text:0131 -->
Decide: close shop or run both?

<!-- wbl-text:0132 -->
Either can work, but you need clarity.

<!-- wbl-text:0133 -->
If closing: orderly wind-down.

<!-- wbl-text:0134 -->
Sell inventory, close vendor accounts, file final BIR returns. Do not do it suddenly.

<!-- wbl-text:0135 -->
If running both: time-block aggressively.

<!-- wbl-text:0136 -->
Mondays + Wednesdays = shop. Tuesdays + Thursdays = freelance. Fridays = both / catch-up.

<!-- wbl-text:0137 -->
📋 The Generalist Admin

<!-- wbl-text:0138 -->
~10% · proceed ~50%

<!-- wbl-text:0139 -->
Steady employee. Transition needs methodical plan.

<!-- wbl-text:0140 -->
Do this week

<!-- wbl-text:0141 -->
Pick a wedge BEFORE resigning.

<!-- wbl-text:0142 -->
Do not rely on "I will figure it out." Decide: Bookkeeping VA, Calendar EA, or another clear wedge.

<!-- wbl-text:0143 -->
Apply to 5 part-time VA roles this month.

<!-- wbl-text:0144 -->
Test market interest before fully committing.

<!-- wbl-text:0145 -->
Setup emergency fund equal to 6 months of expenses.

<!-- wbl-text:0146 -->
Generalist Admins lose to specialists on speed-to-first-client. Buffer longer.
